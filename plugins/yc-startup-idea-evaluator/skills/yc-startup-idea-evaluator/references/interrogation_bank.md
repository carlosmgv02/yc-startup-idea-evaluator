# Interrogation bank

## How to use this file

- Pick 5 to 8 questions for a given idea. Prefer the ones whose answer would move the score by a band; skip anything the founder already answered.
- Ask one at a time in a live conversation. When the answer is vague, ask for a specific story, name or number before moving on. Generic answers are themselves a signal [S17].
- The full banks live next to their doctrine. This file is the router plus the questions that most often decide an evaluation, and a matrix for common founder claims.

## The twelve that decide most evaluations

1. What do you make, for whom, in two sentences? Long or jargon-heavy answers signal muddled thinking or a made-up problem [S01, S17].
2. Who wants this so badly they would use a crappy first version? Name them [S12].
3. What do they do today, and what does it cost them? [S31, S37]
4. How did you come to this idea? Brainstormed ideas are bad and plausible-sounding [S05, S12].
5. Who has tried this before and why did they fail? What changed that hits that reason? [S34, S28]
6. Why now, specifically? A recent change, not "AI is cool" in general [S08, S34]. Some founders argue "because I started now" is enough; weigh it against the evidence [S40].
7. Who writes the code, and what have you built before? [S01, S09]
8. How long have you known each other, and how do you decide when you disagree? [S01, S07]
9. How many real users with no asterisks, and how many love it? [S32]
10. What is your weekly growth as a ratio, and how much is paid or churn-masked? [S08, S26]
11. What unscalable thing will you do in month one to get the first 10 customers? [S06, S53]
12. What is the hard part of this business, the one others avoid? [S11, S75]

## Pushback matrix for common claims

| Founder says | Ask instead | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Everyone we showed it to loves it." | "Who uses it every week without you asking? Who paid?" | Compliments and hypotheticals are rung 0 evidence. | [S31, S37] |
| "The market is $50B." | "How many specific customers can you reach in year one, and what do they pay today?" | Top-down TAM is at most an indicator; beachhead plus path out is what to test. | [S29, S12, S34] |
| "We have no competitors." | "What do people do instead today?" | No competitor often means no problem or no research; competition can be a good sign. | [S12, S34] |
| "We'll grow through virality / SEO / a big launch / a partnership." | "Who are the first 10 users and how will you personally get each one?" | Founders make it take off; launches and partnerships rarely do. | [S06, S01] |
| "We're building the platform for X." | "What is the one thing a small group desperately needs this month?" | Narrow and deep beats broad and shallow. | [S12, S02] |
| "We have 5,000 signups." | "How many came back in week 4?" | Signups without retention is the classic error. | [S01, S44] |
| "We need to raise first, then we'll build." | "What can you put in users' hands in three weeks?" | Launch fast; the MVP is a pretext to engage users. | [S19, S02] |
| "We'll hire a sales person." | "How many deals have the founders closed themselves?" | Sales is the founder's job initially. | [S06] |
| "The model will get better, so we'll be fine." | "If the next model doubles quality, does your product get better or disappear?" | Wrapper value disappears; owned context and outcomes compound. | [S45, S41, S23] |
| "It's an AI copilot for X." | "Which team or budget line does it replace, and who signs?" | Replacing work sells to payroll budget; assisting sells to software budget. | [S23] |
| "Nobody has done this because it's illegal / crazy." | "What exactly is the obstacle, and who is pulling the product out of you anyway?" | Sounds-bad is a signal to test, not a verdict. | [S29, S01] |
| "We're pivoting because growth is slow." | "What did users who stayed tell you? Is this a product problem?" | Slow growth is usually the product [S04, S01]; pivots need evidence [S22, S75]. | [S04, S01, S22, S75] |
