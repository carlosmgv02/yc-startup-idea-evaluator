## One-line thesis
Blomfield's two fintechs show that a bold, hard problem recruits talent, press and customers, that founder-problem fit determines whether you last, and that running a bank brings regulatory, fraud and uptime pressure that scales with success.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Tarpit ideas**: bill splitting is a classic tarpit idea; YC showed the bar was higher and the idea pivoted to B2B payments (GoCardless). Quote: "bills spring idea into a much better B2B payment idea"
- **Founder-problem fit**: he left GoCardless because he could not identify with the customer. Quote: "I'd never run a small business collecting money I couldn't really put myself in the shoes of our customers"
- **Fix something visibly broken**: Quote: "we saw all these problems in the banking space"
- **Incumbent weakness hypothesis**: banks run by people who did not understand software. Quote: "the people running these Banks did not understand what software could do"
- **Prototype in months**: a bank prototype on a prepaid debit card in three or four months. Quote: "we launched a prototype of a bank in about three or four months on a prepaid debit card"
- **Launch early, talk to users, do things that don't scale**: Quote: "launch early and talk to users do things that don't scale"
- **Word-of-mouth by product design**: tap-to-pay triggered a notification on the phone; zero to one million customers in about two and a half years without advertising. Quote: "from zero to a million customers in about 2 and a half years without spending anything on advertising and it was all through Word of Mouth"
- **Hard problems recruit**: ambitious scope attracts talent, press and customers. Quote: "taking on a really hard problem actually makes some things easier so the smartest most ambitious people want to come and work with you"
- **Slow start is normal**: GoCardless had slow growth for a year or two, then compounding. Quote: "growth was pretty slow honestly for the first year or two but we ground away at it"
- **Infrastructure criticality**: payments outages hit paychecks; plan for it. Quote: "if we had an outage even for a few seconds that meant tens of thousands of payments wouldn't go through"
- **Financial crime monitoring is operational work**: Quote: "we spent a lot of time monitoring illegal activity Financial crime money laundering"
- **Revenue concentration risk**: Monzo revenue fell about 50% in COVID because of overseas card use. Quote: "a lot of our Revenue came from people using our card overseas so Revenue dropped by about 50%"
- **Funding can disappear days before closing**: Quote: "this 10000 million funding line that was vital to the survival of the company got pulled 70 2 hours before closing" [sic: figure garbled in captions]

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Is your idea a known tarpit (bill splitting, dating)? — unaware of why prior attempts failed.
- Can you put yourself in your customer's shoes for the next 5 to 10 years? — founder not passionate about the problem.
- What is the smallest prototype you can ship in months, with real users? — plan requires full licence or build first.
- If regulated, what is your plan for fraud, money laundering and outages? — treated as later problems.
- How does the product market itself in use? — growth requires paid acquisition.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Idea in a well-known tarpit without a differentiated angle.
- Founder indifferent to the customer's problem — he left GoCardless after 3 years for this reason.
- Single revenue source exposed to an external shock; a funding round with no cushion.

## Green flags
- Product experience so good customers show friends.
- Big vision that pulls talent, press and customers.
- Fast prototype in the hands of users within months.
- Willingness to take on a "broken" regulated incumbent space.

## Named examples
- GoCardless: B2B payments after the bill-splitting pivot; now tens of billions processed in dozens of countries (per speaker).
- Monzo: started 2015, prepaid debit card prototype, million customers in about 2.5 years, over 8 million customers now (15 to 20% of UK adults), profitable.
- First co-founders' online student marketplace; later YC visiting partner then group partner.

## Tensions
- Nuances "do things that don't scale": the same founder faced a regulated, safety-critical stack where outages and financial crime become the core risk.
- Contradicts "persist at all costs": founder burnout led him to step back from Monzo.
- Source covers fintech regulation only through anecdotes (financial crime monitoring, regulators during COVID); no detailed compliance-as-product advice.
