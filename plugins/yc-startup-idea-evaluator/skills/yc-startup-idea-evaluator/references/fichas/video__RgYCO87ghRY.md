## One-line thesis
Ambition is trained by resetting benchmarks (peers, targets, a "100 million" exercise), but depth and focus on one vertical are what actually deliver it, and growth ambition is a one-way door once you take VC money.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Depth is not lack of ambition**: narrowing produced growth steps. Test whether the founder's focus is a narrowing that multiplies results. Quote: "we focused 10x on photo and we grew 10x and then we focused on e-commerce photo and we grew 10x again"
- **The 100 million ARR exercise**: ask how the business reaches 100M ARR; the answer exposes market size and the most ambitious version. Quote: "explain to me how your business is going to uh lead to 100 million of AR"
- **Add a zero**: take the success metric and add a zero, then work backward. Quote: "just add a zero to whatever success metric you have in mind"
- **Targets become real**: goals get hit because people steer toward them. Quote: "the goals you set yourself they end up happening"
- **Self-fulfilling targets example**: a 10-person offsite set 20M ARR when at a few million and "everyone in the room say like it's not possible and then one year later we're there". Quote: "everyone in the room say like it's not possible and then one year later we're there"
- **Benchmark reset by peers**: talk to founders growing faster than you. Quote: "just by meeting people like this it helps it helps you reset a little bit your your benchmark"
- **Hard problems attract talent**: a tough ambitious problem makes hiring people smarter than you possible. Quote: "if you don't have a hard tough ambitious problem it's going to be very difficult uh to hire smart smarter than you uh people"
- **Growth ambition is a one-way door**: taking VC money commits you to growth every year; decide knowingly. Quote: "one of them is taking VC money and uh signing up for a venture back company because the moment you do this you sign up for more growth every year"
- **Founder alignment on ambition**: ask co-founders and hires what size they want; a disconnect slows the company. Quote: "just this disconnect is enough to slow down the company and just like create fun of frustration"
- **Rank projects by impact**: if it works perfectly, how much revenue. Quote: "ranking all the projects and say okay if this works out perfectly how much revenue it is going to bring us"
- **V0 of everything**: cut each project to the smallest test; with AI days rather than months. Quote: "most of the time like 50% of the project they'll fail like they won't have the impact you expect them to have"
- **Half resources test**: ask what the team would do with half the resources. Quote: "if you had half of the resources what would you do to still achieve this goal"
- **Global from day one as filter**: speaking English in the Paris office filtered for global ambition. Quote: "if you don't like want to be global like you're not going to be ambitious enough"
- **Self-doubt discount**: acknowledge doubts and set them aside. Quote: "It's very useful to erase those dots you will have them for sure but just set them aside"
- **Postmortems on projects**: look at where the decision chain failed so next time you pick a more ambitious project. Quote: "what piece in the decision chain didn't go well so that next time I'm not going to make that that mistake"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- How does this reach 100M ARR, concretely? — Cannot describe a path, or the market caps out far below.
- Would you pick slower growth on a project you love, or fast growth on something else? — Not thought through, and has taken or wants VC money.
- What did you narrow on last, and what multiple did it give? — Breadth without depth.
- Do your co-founders want the same size of outcome? — Divergent answers.
- What is the V0 of this project, and what would you learn in three days? — Months-long first version.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Targets set only from the founder's own immediate circle, with no peers growing faster.
- Founders misaligned on ambition (mission versus scale).
- Taking venture money without wanting growth ("a one-way door").
- Accepting "not possible" from friends without testing.

## Green flags
- Fastest growth in the batch (zero to 1 million during the batch in the Photoroom story).
- Focus that produced successive 10x steps.
- Small team with global distribution from start.

## Named examples
- Photoroom: AI visuals for e-commerce; 300 million downloads, 20 million active users, Amazon and others as users (per the speaker).
- Front (YC summer 2014): founder was unsure early, later top company.
- Marathon finishing-time spikes at 2:30 and 3 hours as evidence targets shape results.
- Satellite founders at a YC dinner: peers in a 20 to 30 person company launching satellites.

## Tensions
- A speaker offers a contrarian view: ambition is not always necessary if you prefer a passion project, versus the classic VC model that requires growth.
- Cultural claim (Europeans less ambitious) is anecdotal, based on the speakers' own hiring experience.
- Ambition via targets contrasts with the same session's advice to go deep and narrow.
