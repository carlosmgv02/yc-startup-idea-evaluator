## One-line thesis
Good AI startup ideas come either from looking within (deep expertise earned at the edge of a field) or from getting out of the house (embedding in real businesses and their boring problems), not from hackathon ideas, trends or feeds.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Lazy hackathon idea**: default bad idea is a bandwagon or something easy to build. Quote: "the default uh bad startup idea is lazy and that it's almost like a hackathon idea"
- **Harder is better**: Quote: "most of the best startup ideas are actually like at least somewhat hard to actually ship the first version of"
- **Subconscious pull to the easy**: Quote: "their subconscious is trying to push them back in the direction of things that they can build in like a weekend"
- **Look within or outside**: Quote: "you need to aggressively get out of the house into other places"
- **Expertise first in pivots**: Quote: "are the founders experts in anything because the founders are experts in anything then like often that's the place to look for for ideas first"
- **Founders do not know their own expertise**: Quote: "it's often surprisingly hard for the founders to actually know what they themselves are experts in"
- **Allergic reaction to prior domain**: Quote: "they want to chase some shiny object that they don't know anything about sort of the grass is greener"
- **Edge-of-field fit**: Quote: "there's no one like like them that had that work experience and that happened to want to do a startup"
- **If not us then who**: Quote: "if not us then who"
- **Internships and where you work**: Quote: "some huge percentage of y's billion dollar companies can be traced directly back to not just a job but specifically an internship"
- **No domain experience, hard to build for**: Uncle GPT/Datacurve founders had not been product managers. Quote: "very very hard to make software or products for"
- **Capture the imagination**: Quote: "who cares work on something that captures the human imagination"
- **Blinders**: Quote: "your subconscious doesn't even allow you to see certain ideas because they seem too ambitious and too scary"
- **Build expertise like a researcher**: Quote: "start treating yourself as researchers"
- **Access is the unlock**: family or friend connections into underserved industries. Quote: "access is all you need actually"
- **AI makes small niches big**: replacing a $60-80k human, not a CRM seat. Quote: "now it's like any one of these things with AI you it's so much more valuable than just building like a CRM for a dentist office"
- **Fly on the wall**: shadow a day in an industry. Quote: "anytime you can find anyone working in any industry who will let you do that is freaking gold"
- **Undercover job**: take a remote knowledge-work job to learn it. Quote: "I want to automate medical billing but in order to do that I need to understand how it works so I'm going to get a job as a medical biller"
- **Job-board mining**: Quote: "you can literally go to indeed.com and search for jobs that have keywords like this like remote analyst remote like clerk"
- **Shadow a friend with a boring job**: Quote: "think of if you have any friends who have very boring jobs um and go follow them and Shadow them at work for a day"
- **Outsourced labor signal**: Quote: "is like uh a strong signal that there is a startup to be built right now"
- **Consultant-dependent products**: Quote: "there's a bunch of Consultants that are making a bunch of money to just get that product to work"
- **Live at the edge**: use the latest tools to notice what is possible. Quote: "live at the edge and notice What's missing"
- **Smart builder friends**: Quote: "you would only find out if you end up working with the top percentile of Builders"
- **Ship anything to build expertise**: Quote: "the expertise they built was because they just launched something"
- **Ignore feed-driven investor doubt**: Quote: "you have direct knowledge of the world out there"
- **Competitive space is not disqualifying**: Giga ML entered crowded customer support and won through technical delivery. Quote: "very very few people can actually deliver to the level that customers want"
- **Idea search can take a year**: Quote: "it took them about a year if I don't recall to find the right idea and that's quite normal actually"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What are you an expert in, and what did you see at your last job or internship? — "nothing", or a domain they are fleeing.
- Why can you build this better than anyone else (n equals one)? — no unique experience.
- Have you spent a day sitting in the user's workplace? — only desk research or X posts.
- What did you ship to real users and what did they say or pay? — only a demo.
- Is this idea easy to build in a weekend? Why not harder? — the idea is a hackathon-level wrapper.
- Is the work already being done by humans, or outsourced to low-wage countries? — no existing labor budget.
- What would break if the AI part failed? — only a demo exists; no reliability.
- Who are your connections into this industry? — none, nobody to give access.
- What is the bigger version if it works 10x? — idea is inconsequential.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Hackathon or ChatGPT-wrapper idea: "really cool demo but there weren't customers that were willing to pay".
- Chasing hot ideas from X or Tech Crunch announcements.
- Founders building for professionals they have never been.
- Idea chosen because it is easy to ship.
- Manufactured B2B SaaS ideas with no excitement.
- Dropping a domain you are expert in for a shiny object.
- Psyching out because "two competitors launched on TechCrunch".

## Green flags
- Founders with prior jobs or internships at the edge (Tesla finance ops, Figma front end, Cohere data work).
- Rare skill intersections (software plus hardware engineer).
- Family or friend access into an industry.
- Founder went undercover or shadowed (dentist office, medical biller, police ride-alongs).
- Work that is currently routine clerical and AI can automate.
- Strong technical delivery in a crowded category where few can actually deliver.

## Named examples
- Salient: ex-Tesla finance ops founder, voice agent for auto loan collections.
- Diode Computer: AI circuit board copilot, founders both EE and software.
- Spur: AI QA agent, founder from Figma.
- Datacurve (ex Uncle GPT): 19-year-old Waterloo dropouts; pivot to data for LLMs via Cohere internship; "mid to high seven figures".
- David AI: ex-Scale, multimodal speaker-separated audio data.
- Can of Soup: founder told to "work on something that captures the human imagination" (AI Instagram-like).
- Happenstance: Apple AI researcher, LLM plus vector search for people search.
- EasyDubs: real-time universal translator (example of beating blinders).
- ESS Health: dentist office admin and insurance work after founder shadowed his mother.
- Happy Robot: logistics AI agents; founders Spanish PhD students, got in through being personable.
- Undisclosed AI billing company: cofounder took a real medical biller job.
- Able Police: police paperwork; ride-alongs.
- Sweet Spot: AI for government contract bidding after a friend's refresh-the-page job.
- Lilac Labs: drive-thru order takers outsourced overseas.
- Automat: RPA without consultants (vs UiPath).
- PreDB: Postgres/pgvector replacing separate vector DBs.
- Reducto: PDF chunk extraction for RAG, found via builder friends.
- Juicebox (People GPT): freelancer marketplace to recruiter search.
- Giga ML: edtech to fine-tuning to customer support; won Zepto.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the "expertise is required" frame by showing ex-influencers can build expertise by shipping.
- Pivot guidance: look for expertise first, yet founders are often allergic to their own expertise.
- Seed extensions and year-long idea searches are now normal, versus "ancient history" when seed funds refused extensions.
- Expertise-first advice for 19-year-olds with no domain experience is hard (the Datacurve story shows going back to an internship).
- Undercover job idea is legal only because the AI ran locally on the founder's own computer; the speakers do not generalize this beyond that case.
