## One-line thesis
Stop searching for the perfect idea: pick one, burn the other boats, become a deep expert by working with customers, and validate with pull plus three AI-era qualities (edge of model capability, verticalized outcome, most ambitious version).

## Evaluation heuristics
- **No perfect idea in the abstract**: ideas are found by contact with reality. Quote: "it's impossible to figure out the perfect idea in the abstract you can only figure out what you should be working on by making contact with reality and getting feedback"
- **Founder-market fit is not a decade of experience**: curiosity plus depth plus customers can substitute. Quote: "if you pick an idea you're curious about go extremely deep and most importantly talk to customers"
- **Multiple ideas produce bad data**: juggling gives false negatives and false positives. Quote: "it tends to produce bad data"
- **Burn the other boats**: explicit foreclosure of alternatives, including telling customers you pivoted. Quote: "you should explicitly foreclose your other startup idea options stop working on them tell any customers that you've pivoted"
- **New skin test**: depth shows as a changed identity (name, emails, narrative). Quote: "it should feel like wearing a new skin you should become an almost unrecognizable version of yourself"
- **Run-their-business test**: the high watermark of depth. Quote: "could you actually run your customers business"
- **Unanswered operational questions**: daily crises, top five problems, cost of an unanswered call, what they would pay. Quote: "do you know how much business they lose when a call goes unanswered and what they would actually pay to never lose another one"
- **Could-you-teach-a-class test**: Quote: "could you teach a class on the problem you're solving are you one of the most informed people in the world on the subject"
- **Tight loop**: do not wait for hundreds of interviews before code; alternate understanding and delivery. Quote: "deep understanding of customer needs then product delivery then deeper understanding of customer needs then better product delivery"
- **Edge-of-models test**: the idea barely works on today's frontier models but will clearly improve with them. Quote: "the idea sits at the edge of what models can do today"
- **Bottleneck-as-company**: know what limits your product; solving it may be the company. Quote: "if a particular bottleneck doesn't clear the way you hoped solving that might become the company"
- **Verticalize to an outcome**: sell insurance or medical care rather than software for it. Quote: "the things that actually become valuable aren't just software for X they're customer trust licenses regulatory permission and outcome ownership"
- **Be the insurer, be the bank**: Quote: "don't build software for insurance companies just be the insurer"
- **Most ambitious version costs the same**: a modest and a wildly ambitious startup are both extremely hard. Quote: "the cost of pursuing a wildly ambitious startup idea and the cost of pursuing a modest one are roughly the same"
- **Ambition as moat**: the ambitious version protects against competitors and attracts talent. Quote: "that's also the version that protects you from competitors attracts the best talent and has a moat worth building"
- **Depth finds the better idea**: opportunities are structural problems underneath surface pain. Quote: "going deep isn't primarily a process for validating the idea you started with it's a way to find the better idea underneath"
- **Cost of indecision**: the worst failure mode is inaction. Quote: "the worst failure mode isn't being wrong it's not making a decision"
- **Walk fast in the fog**: sampling a little everywhere yields almost no information. Quote: "you generate much more information per unit of time"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Which other ideas have you stopped working on, and what did you tell those customers? — Still working on several.
- If I dropped you into your customer's business tomorrow, could you run it? — Cannot name daily crises or the cost of the problem.
- What breaks in your product on today's best model, and does it improve with the next one? — Works only with a model that will not improve, or already works fully.
- Are you selling software or the outcome? What license or trust do you own? — Software for X only.
- What is the version of this that rewrites a sector? — Modest scope chosen to stay safe.
- What did you learn from users that changed the idea? — Nothing changed.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Waiting for the best idea before committing.
- Using lack of domain experience as a reason not to start.
- Running several ideas at once to "compare".
- Surface-level pain points that are not structural.
- Product that is just software while the value migrates to trust, licenses and outcomes.

## Green flags
- Single-minded focus with visible identity change after a pivot (GovDash changed name and emails each pivot).
- Founder who can teach a class on the customer's problem.
- Idea at the edge of what models can do and improving.
- Full-stack ownership (underwriting to customer service).
- Aiming at regulated industries, big incumbents or hard tech.

## Named examples
- Blake Scholl (Boom Supersonic): ad-tech background, built supersonic flight company, illustrating no domain prerequisite.
- GovDash: pivoted at least five times; fifth idea about government procurement worked and raised a Series B.
- Corgi Insurance (YC summer 24): acquired an insurance carrier during the batch to own the full commercial insurance stack.
- Voice customer service agents for cleaning services: example used for the run-their-business test.

## Tensions
- Contradicts "test many ideas in parallel to find the best one".
- Pushes against a standard founder-market-fit requirement, while conceding that a non-technical founder is unlikely to be the right person for a DevTools idea.
- "Don't obsess over hundreds of customer conversations before code" versus "know the customer's business well enough to run it": resolved by the tight loop.
- "Sell an outcome, not software" assumes capital and regulatory capacity many early founders do not have.
