## One-line thesis
Luminai moved from a horizontal automation platform to healthcare-only, wedge use cases (starting with faxed referrals), and sells to large hospitals through a champion reached by warm introductions and a personal mission story.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Pivot method is methodical**: combine a mission that energizes you with deep customer pain, then generalize. Quote: "it can be quite methodical."
- **Mission fit for the long haul**: Quote: "is there a mission or story that truly energizes me to work on a problem for a very long time?"
- **Operational teams doing manual workflows is the wedge**: in large enterprises the pain is repeated manual process. Quote: "this whole problem of, uh, operational teams mostly doing deeply manual workflows became very evident."
- **Pick a concrete, ugly workflow**: faxed referrals to Cleveland Clinic: triage spam vs critical patient, extract data, match patient and provider in the EHR, route to department, start scheduling. Quote: "every single fax that comes in hits Lumina first."
- **Data transformation layer plus workflow engine**: structure unstructured data first, then verticalized agents. Quote: "we're converting all of this unstructured data like faxes into structured data. And then we have a workflow engine on top"
- **Size by waste pool**: Quote: "the 30% of spend that's on the administrative waste within health care, which is over a trillion dollars"
- **You sell to a champion, not an institution**: find forward-leaning individuals inside the customer. Quote: "you're not actually selling a customer like the Cleveland Clinic. You're selling a champion within that one institution"
- **Warm introductions for big institutions**: no cold email for the first years; map second and third degree connections. Quote: "really major institutions uh don't don't really talk to you unless it's someone who they trust is like making the referral."
- **Method to find referrers**: script that scrapes investors' LinkedIn contacts and matches them to the target customer profile. Quote: "scrape every single contact of theirs and then just match it against our like target customer profile."
- **Early enterprise sale = a bet on the team**: Quote: "they have to take a bet on you."
- **Lead with personal story and mission**: Quote: "when you lead with your personal story and when you lead with your like um your history in life and you lead with your mission"
- **Narrow focus raises credibility**: when 80% of customers were in healthcare, they dropped horizontal. Quote: "the moment you start to say I can do anything or I work with someone else who doesn't look like you at all, the credibility you have reduces pretty dramatically."
- **Wedge use cases with quick time to value**: large institutions do not think creatively about your platform. Quote: "You have to almost tell the story of what's possible very very clearly"
- **Keep reinventing at each stage**: Quote: "with every stage you have to reinvent yourself."
- **Rapid feedback loops**: at hackathons, 16 iteration loops with organizers who became judges. Quote: "you end up building exactly what the hackathon organizers wanted who then eventually became the judges"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Who is the named champion inside the first customer, and how did you reach them? — "we will do cold outbound to the institution."
- Which single workflow is the wedge, and how fast does it show value? — platform pitch with no starting use case.
- What share of your customers is in one vertical? — spread across industries, credibility diluted (Luminai: north of 80% healthcare).
- Why would a risk-averse institution take a bet on you? — nothing beyond product features.
- What is the manual process, who does it today and how many people? — cannot describe the operational team.

## Red flags / kill signals
- "I can do anything" horizontal automation pitch to enterprise buyers — credibility drops with each unrelated claim.
- First idea with no customer pull: developer documentation; the interviewer said "your idea is not great" and it failed after three weeks of customer iteration.
- Selling to an institution rather than a person.

## Green flags
- Customer base clustering in one vertical without planning it.
- Customers referring other customers and advisors championing the company.
- Founder willing to fly anywhere the next morning to meet a buyer.
- Existing deployments in hospitals processing real fax volume.

## Named examples
- Luminai: Series B of $38 million from Peak 15; raised about $60 million; "AI transformation partner for health systems."
- Cleveland Clinic: little over 16 million patient encounters last year; referral by fax.
- Earlier horizontal automation platform, documentation tool for engineers (pivoted away within three weeks).

## Tensions
- Contradicts "send cold emails and iterate": warm introductions were the main channel for big institutions.
- Contradicts "enterprise sales is about product features": the person and mission narrative gets the meeting; the product must still deliver value.
- Nothing in this source covers regulation or compliance (HIPAA etc.) as a product or a barrier; do not infer it.
