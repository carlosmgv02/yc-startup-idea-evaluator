## One-line thesis
Use "Proven, Better, New" to isolate a single untested hypothesis, expect the new part to fail, read true signal ("the fish are running"), stay in founder mode, and build consumer AI now by designing for the cost curve of intelligence falling.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Proven, Better, New**: Copy what is proven without questioning, make it measurably better (every existing user would say so), and add only one new, probably wrong hypothesis. Check that the idea isolates one untested instinct. Quote: "we do all the proven mechanics of it and then the only new thing is that it's always on listening but we start off with the proposition that that idea is probably wrong"
- **Better must be unanimous**: Better means something 10 out of 10 existing users would call better (free, half price, faster). Quote: "better would be something that 10 out of 10 existing users would say better"
- **Expect new to fail**: Plan for none of the new ideas catching; the new feature gets trial, not retention. Quote: "assume none i mean new is like the novel idea that will get someone interested in your product"
- **Trial vs retention**: The new feature is the back of the box that gets the first purchase; the proven product is why they come back. Quote: "it's probably not what what you came back for like you come back for the the granola"
- **Death of the ego**: Stay passionate about the instinct and dispassionate about the product variant. Quote: "how do we stay passionate about the the instinct that we're pursuing the vision but dispassionate about this particular product variant and idea"
- **True signal**: When the product is right, every feedback loop is a clear yes and no one has to be told to work faster; when it is not, metrics are debatable. Quote: "when you've got that heat that true signalance right you don't have to tell anyone to work harder or faster like everyone sees it"
- **Go slow to go fast**: Get to real conviction first and then move fast. Quote: "I think have to go slow to go fast like get to real conviction and then everyone is going to move fast against it"
- **Collect winnings, not make bets**: Great product makers see the hit before launch. Quote: "great product makers you know they're collecting winnings not making bets"
- **Investors can be 180 degrees off**: Investors pushed a team with excellent consumer metrics to pivot to enterprise because it is more fundable. Quote: "the investors you know they're sometimes exactly 180 degrees off they're not thinking about first principles at all"
- **Models are in-distribution for proven**: An LLM grades "proven" at A minus, "better" at B minus and "new" at D; humans are for new. Quote: "it's in distribution you know it's literally in the model sometimes right"
- **Distribution is the unknown for consumer**: No proven distribution path in consumer now; look for a viral hook. Quote: "there is no proven path for distribution right now in I would argue in consumer which is why it's so hard"
- **Founder mode**: Do not abdicate to the board or investors; "don't be an expert witness in your own company". Quote: "we all start as expert witnesses in early in our careers that we're closest to the answer and furthest from the decision"
- **Be in the room**: Management tools exist so people do the right thing when you are not there. Quote: "only point of all of these management tools is to get people to do the right thing when we're not in the room"
- **CEO must love the product**: Quote: "i don't trust the CEO of a consumer company that doesn't love their product and doesn't know their product better than anybody else"
- **Cost-curve timing**: Take a cost curve and imagine the product when it is free; consumer is the app that costs about $1,000 a month today. Quote: "how would I use it if if I could just use unlimited amounts for everything all the time"
- **Free as the business model**: Freeloader, Zynga and free-to-play all used free where the incumbent charged. Quote: "anything that can be free will be free"
- **Be early and survive the wait**: Timing can take almost a decade (Amazon's numbers only climbed from late 2002). Quote: "be wrong for like almost a decade and then suddenly everyone's like "Oh you were so right.""
- **The Abyss**: Gaps between products are where you widen taste. Quote: "the abyss is this place that in between passionately pursuing products that we find ourselves in"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What is proven, what is better, and what is the one new bet? — Several unproven hypotheses at once.
- Would all of your existing customers call it better? — Only some, or none asked.
- If the new feature fails, does the product still stand? — Whole product rests on the new idea.
- Why would investors say pivot to enterprise, and why do you disagree? — Pivots because it is fundable.
- What does this product look like when intelligence is free? — Only designed for today's cost.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Several unproven hypotheses tested at once — the framework isolates the new.
- Attachment to a product variant — ego blocks pivots.
- Consumer idea with no viral hook or distribution plan.

## Green flags
- Product signal where "every feedback loop is a fuck yes".
- Excellent consumer metrics despite investor pressure (an office-hours company).
- Founder who knows the product better than anyone and is present in details.

## Named examples
- Zynga: free social games versus the $60 boxed model; Freeloader: free interactive screensaver vs $35 flying toasters, 2 million downloads in the first month.
- Tribe: failed social network because the trust container was wrong; Facebook succeeded via .edu trust.
- Granola, Gemini Live voice, Siri: examples of always-on AI listening as a possible consumer opportunity.
- Peter Steinberger: spends large sums on tokens to build OpenClaw (speaker's claims of a million dollars a year or more).

## Tensions
- Contradicts "enterprise is the fundable place": says consumer opportunity has never been greater though "arguably not investable right now".
- The speaker admits managing by changing his mind each Monday ("third grade soccer") is poor management unless the context is set at multiple altitudes.
- Claims enterprise AI spend shows no benefit for 90% of companies (a stat the speaker saw) and calls it a "skill issue" for now.
