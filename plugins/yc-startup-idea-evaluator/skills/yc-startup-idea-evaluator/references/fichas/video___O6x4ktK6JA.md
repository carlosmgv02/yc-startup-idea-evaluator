## One-line thesis
OpenCode grew by taking the neutral, open position between two dominant closed coding agents, betting on the whole field of models, and using free tokens as the new customer acquisition cost, after 16 years and many pivots inside one legal entity.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Take the open alternative to the duopoly**: When one or two players dominate, the rest of the market coalesces around an open option; if you take that slot early it is hard to displace. Check whether the founder deliberately chose that position. Quote: "when you've got a dominant or in this case two dominant players in the market the rest of the market coaleses around an open alternative"
- **Bet the field, not a winner**: Test whether the business gains from every model lab improving (marketplace of models) rather than depending on one. Quote: "it's not that we're picking a winner in terms of a model lab We're just betting the field"
- **Token cost is the new CAC**: In AI products, acquisition spend moves from ads to free tokens that deliver the magic moment. Check cost per activated user. Quote: "rather than paying for ads is paying for tokens"
- **Free tier to aha moment to subscription to whales**: Funnel logic: free tier gets the aha, a cheap plan allows real work, a fraction become high spenders who pay per token. Quote: "a certain percentage of them cross it get to that point are spending the the crazy amounts"
- **Unit economics from volume discounts**: Per-token pay-through can become margin via volume discounts, unlike subsidised flat plans. Quote: "when you pay per token that effectively turns into our margin"
- **PMF signal in enterprise**: Inbound security questionnaires from companies where employees already use the product before sales ever spoke to them. Quote: "You really know you have product market fit when like enterprises are bugging you to sign the security agreement so they can use your product"
- **Bottom-up adoption**: Look for usage inside large companies that the founder did not know existed. Quote: "I didn't know we had users there"
- **Own the user data**: A product that sees per-user, cross-model usage has an insight aggregators lack (they only see aggregate). Quote: "we get to see actual usage data for each of the users as opposed to with maybe like an open router where you're seeing it aggregated"
- **Global usage smooths unit costs**: Users across time zones flatten GPU utilisation peaks, a cost advantage over single-region peers. Quote: "we have a reasonably stable 24-hour GPU cycle which allows for pretty good utilization"
- **Competitor missteps as growth events**: A rival clamping down on third-party use equated the two products and drove awareness. Quote: "it put open code and clawed code on the same sort of pedestal it like equated the two products"
- **Unlock timing from model parity**: Growth followed the moment open-weight models became good enough for real work (a four-week span where one open model out-used the frontier ones). Quote: "that's the point when we were like oh I think we should launch a subscription product because now maybe these models actually do make sense for real work"
- **Product taste for core users**: The founders built the experience their own community (neovim users) respected, from earlier goofy projects. Quote: "if we built one of those it would resonate with them almost right away"
- **Build in public as identity**: Public metrics and code created a following. Quote: "if you don't talk about it publicly you're probably doing yourself a disservice and your product a disservice"
- **Accumulated skills position you for the wave**: Years of pivots built marketing, consumer acquisition and positioning skills that mattered when the opening came. Quote: "to catch lightning in the bottle you actually like have to sort of position the bottle correctly and be ready for it"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Which two incumbents dominate and why is there a gap for the neutral alternative? — No dominant players or no reason users want choice.
- What is your cost to give a user the aha moment and what percent convert to paid? — No measured funnel.
- Who is using the product inside big companies without talking to you? — All usage is from your own outreach.
- What breaks if a model lab changes its terms or pricing? — Single-supplier dependence.
- What do you know from your usage data that nobody else can see? — Nothing proprietary.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Dependence on a single vendor's subscription being usable inside your product — Anthropic tried to block it by checking the system prompt for the word "open code".
- Subsidising usage without a path to margin — the source contrasts subsidy with per-token pass-through margin; Anthropic and OpenAI can subsidise, a startup mostly cannot (speaker says they subsidise "a little bit").

## Green flags
- Fast organic enterprise pull before any sales motion; Fortune 500 users who ask not to be named publicly.
- Growth traceable to specific upstream events (new open-source model releases).
- Founders still together after a decade who treat each pivot as learning.

## Named examples
- OpenCode (SST/Anomaly legal entity incorporated in 2010): weekly active users 4.6 million; about 13 million monthly active at end of June, "around a 20x increase since the beginning of the year"; about 7 trillion tokens per day vs OpenRouter around 6 trillion (speaker's figures); about 160,000 monthly subscribers, about 18 million annualised revenue from subscriptions.
- Ramp: built an internal Slack bot running OpenCode's agent loop server separately from the UI.
- Instacart (host example): the "death of Instacart" meme after Amazon bought Whole Foods drove grocers to check it out.
- terminal.shop: goofy side project (order coffee over SSH) cited as proof that eccentric taste leads to outlying outcomes.

## Tensions
- Contradicts "stay focused on one customer segment": founder says OpenCode addresses consumers, open source, mid-market and enterprise "all together", justified by having tried each.
- Challenges "quit and join a high-growth startup": founder says the prudent path was to shut down, and warns "don't try this at home"; survival relied on living with parents.
- Open-source and commoditised models argue the app/harness layer captures value, against the "frontier labs take all the margin" view.
