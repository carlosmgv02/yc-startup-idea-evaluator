## One-line thesis
Agent-generated product and landing design makes everyone look the same and untrustworthy; design is a differentiator, and a tool or product built natively for agents (code as source of truth) plus human taste wins.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Do the thing you would do anyway (founder-market fit)**: Quote: "You should do the thing you're going to do anyway You should do the thing where you have founder market fit"
- **Agent-native architecture is a technical wedge**: build on formats agents already know to cut token spend and errors. Quote: "historically a lot of design tools have custom rendering engines and those uh are a lot harder for agents to use and so you'll see higher token spend slower times lower accuracy"
- **Good human handoff is good agent handoff**: Quote: "if you build a great handoff for humans you've also built a great handoff for agents"
- **Code as single source of truth**: two copies of a design system cannot be kept in sync. Quote: "see so many teams maintain two copies of their design system"
- **Design as differentiator**: Quote: "if you look at every great company of the last 10 20 years basically all of them have exceptional design"
- **Looking vibe-coded erodes trust, worst in regulated verticals**: Quote: "this feels you know I don't know if I trust this brand right now And this is a area where you have to have trust"
- **Above-the-fold clarity**: the value prop must be visible without scrolling. Quote: "I had no idea what it was until we got to the animation"
- **Avoid the common AI design tells**: bold weights, many font sizes, purple gradients, cards, tiny all-caps headers, light/dark mode MVP. Quote: "try to work with three if you get down to three"
- **Widgets for widgets' sake**: Quote: "a lot of it turns out to be widgets for widgets sake"
- **Marketing budget as product-adjacent assets**: build open-source libraries/fonts instead of buying ads. Quote: "we think of it instead of buying ads let's make this useful library for the world"
- **Values-aligned audience before product**: Quote: "Being values aligned with your market They will forgive so much about your product missing things"
- **Bottlenecks first**: Quote: "bottlenecks first huge huge philosophy I really believe in"
- **Talk to users daily**: Quote: "I had to talk to a designer every single day and I did it all the way through the year"
- **Quality as positioning versus feature-rich competitors**: Quote: "we have competitors that have every single feature but they don't have the attention or people don't care"
- **Small elite team**: Quote: "if you have less people you have less communication burden"
- **Models learn tactics, not org decisions**: Quote: "a lot of design is actually a very human task of like decision-m in an organization"
- **Free giveaway when no venture-scale path**: Quote: "we just didn't see a way to build like a venture scale business around it and so we thought let's let's just make it free and give it away"
- **Market clock**: Quote: "Every company eventually goes up market and you just stop being as cool when you do that"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Does your product look like the default output of a coding agent? — Red flag: purple gradients, many font weights, generic pills; no intentional brand.
- What do users understand above the fold in 5 seconds? — Red flag: explanation only after the animation or scroll.
- Where is your source of truth and are two copies drifting? — Red flag: separate design and code systems maintained by hand.
- How do you reach your market before the product is complete? — Red flag: no audience or values signal.
- What is your current bottleneck and what releases it? — Red flag: working on features that are not the constraint.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Shipping the first thing the model produces in a trust-sensitive category (health, payments).
- Agent-scale designs with no human control layer: "you can't vibe code a design tool at the level of a Figma today" (his own constraint).
- Features everywhere with no care for quality.

## Green flags
- Founder's strongest founder-market fit and sustained contact with users.
- Community growth from authenticity (25,000 Twitter followers before a product, his figure).
- Competitor-share evidence (Ramp data shows Paper next to Sketch, as he says).

## Named examples
- Paper: HTML/CSS agent-native design tool, 12 people; Paper Shaders open library; Paper Mono font; MCP server in desktop app.
- Modulz / Radix UI: free component library; sold to WorkOS [as spoken].
- Legion Health, Sciteex [sic], Moretta: sites reviewed; critique on trust, clarity above the fold.
- Conductor, Cursor, Claude Code: the "new agent stack" with a visual layer.
- Quarter posters: brand designers using shaders.

## Tensions
- Product-side: his team still reads every line of code and avoids agents in Slack ("dinosaurs") while selling an agent-native tool.
- Predicts models will fix tactical tells over time, while design as stakeholder decision-making is "maybe 10 years" away (his guess).
- Contradicts speed-at-all-costs advice for early founders: he argues against shipping default AI design even with "zero time".
