## One-line thesis
Product market fit is the hardest and most company-killing step (idea to traction); find a hidden need, iterate with customers on their problems rather than their solutions, measure returning usage, NPS and renewals, and do not scale the team until you have it.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **PMF is the hard part, not monetization**: Check that the plan to reach real user pull is credible before worrying about revenue. Quote: "monetization is actually a much easier problem than product market fit"
- **Two hard things**: Finding PMF and hiring a world-class team; making money is a distant third. Quote: "it is usually a lot harder to build a product that a lot of people really really want than it is to figure out how to make money"
- **Hidden need**: The need should not be obvious; if it were, everyone would do it. Quote: "if it was obvious then everyone would be doing it and so so so the hardest part is finding this hidden need"
- **Everyone says it is dumb**: A dismissed idea can be a signal. Quote: "everyone is going to tell you that the idea is dumb everyone's gonna tell you it's stupid"
- **Create a market, so market research fails**: Quote: "market research is research on existing markets right and you're going to create a new market"
- **Job to be done and substitutes**: Define the job and its substitutes. Quote: "what are you a substitute for what need are you serving better what job are people hiring you to do"
- **People do not want the tool, they want the outcome**: Quote: "people aren't trying to make a website people are trying to launch and grow their business"
- **Pull, not push**: Look for customers pulling you; when they hack your product, double down. Quote: "when you find people hacking your product to do something that wasn't intended to do pay attention to that and double down on that"
- **Less functionality, new entrants**: The first product may look like a weaker version of an existing category but enable a new market. Quote: "it enables a whole new market whole set of new entrants to come and use your product"
- **Problems, not solutions**: Quote: "listen to their problems not their solutions"
- **Iteration count**: Expect many cycles. Quote: "expect it'll take about ten times that many iterations"
- **Burn must cover 20 to 30 iterations**: Quote: "if you only have enough cash in the bank for two to three iterations and it's going to take you 20 to 30 then that's not gonna work"
- **Do not outsource early build**: Quote: "when you outsource a project that's one shot well if it's gonna take you 20 to 30 shots not gonna work"
- **Prototype before building**: Quote: "building a fully functional product just to figure out if it's going to work or not is a really really expensive way to test the hypothesis"
- **Do not scale early**: Quote: "don't worry about scaling until you need to"
- **Target customer but flexible**: Talk to the target customer, yet update who the target is if others hack it. Quote: "make sure that you are super flexible on changing your view on who those target customers are"
- **Do not overthink**: Quote: "anecdotal is okay just getting in there and building and launching something and trying it out and iterating is the most important thing you can do"
- **Three tools**: 5 to 10 customer interviews, 3 to 5 UX sessions, metrics. Quote: "customer interviews you probably need about five to ten of these"
- **Metrics caution**: Small samples mislead. Quote: "be very careful about telling too many stories with your metrics that maybe aren't supported by the statistical significance"
- **UX session discipline**: Observe in person without speaking. Quote: "you are not allowed to touch the phone or keyboard"
- **Minimum remarkable product**: Quote: "minimum remarkable product the product that is the the least you can build to be remarkable"
- **Launch when better**: Quote: "launch when your product is better than what's out there"
- **One thing matters**: Quote: "focus only on the thing that gets you to that next stage"
- **Optimize for learning**: For discontinuous improvement, pick the biggest unknown. Quote: "ask yourself what is my biggest unknown right now what's the number-one thing that I do not know"
- **Returning usage**: Quote: "look at the number of people who come back within a day within three days than seven days within 30 days"
- **Friends and family do not return**: Quote: "those are the people who liked you the most and those are the people who would really want you to be successful"
- **NPS threshold**: Quote: "if it's above 50 you're doing pretty well"
- **Renewal rates over churn**: Quote: "your renewal rates are great cuz that's looking for the number the percentage of people who are eligible to renew and what percentage those people actually renewed"
- **Ignore signups early**: Quote: "I would not pay that much attention to signups I would pay a lot of attention to active users"
- **Conversion rate early**: Quote: "conversion rates they all start low they build over time"
- **Feel of PMF**: Quote: "when you have it the whole world is beating a path to your door"
- **Product, channel, model fit**: Quote: "product market Channel model fit"
- **Price must match channel**: Quote: "if you're gonna have a sales force you're probably need to charge people at least two or three hundred dollars a month"
- **Team size before PMF**: Quote: "I would not scale past about 20 people"
- **Scale after PMF but at most double per year**: Quote: "you should probably never more than double the size your company in any given year"
- **Customers over competitors**: Not a feature grid. Quote: "customers over competitors"
- **Win on one job**: Quote: "your product does that one thing that one job they're trying to it does that better than anyone else"
- **Brand from an insight**: Quote: "great brands are built around a fundamental insight a consumer insight that some truth that's just not really acknowledged out there"
- **Fundraising timing**: Quote: "most companies will not be able to raise money until they're at the early traction phase"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What hidden need do you see that others do not, and what job is the customer hiring you for? — an obvious, widely recognized need.
- What are the substitutes for that job today? — "there are none" with no investigation.
- Of people who sign up, what share return within 1, 3, 7 and 30 days? — only signups tracked.
- Are customers using the product in ways you did not intend? — no observation of usage.
- How many iteration cycles can your runway fund? — two or three.
- What is your biggest unknown right now and how are you ordering work to learn it? — a priority list sorted by cost times benefit only.
- What is your NPS or renewal rate by cohort? — not measured.
- Does your price match your acquisition channel? — a middle price with neither sales force nor credit-card impulse.
- Why would you hire beyond about 20 people before PMF? — scaling headcount ahead of fit.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Signups rising while returning usage is low — "signups basically like completely drops off and you have very little active users".
- Pushing customers towards a solution — "they're too nice to tell you no but they're not really coming back".
- Outsourcing the build — one shot instead of 20 to 30.
- Obsessing over scaling code or monetization before product experience works.
- Mid-price product with no matching channel (about $75 a month: too expensive for impulse, too cheap for sales force).
- Scaling the team before PMF; around 23 to 25 people flat structure breaks.
- Watching competitors' feature grids — "you're inherently following".
- Using unsupported A/B test claims with tiny sample sizes.

## Green flags
- Customers hack the product to do something unintended.
- Friends, family and early users return on their own.
- NPS above 50; renewal rates high.
- Early signals come from a new set of users rather than an incumbent's.
- Everything feels easy: press, users arriving unprompted.
- Many iterations with customers, low burn, small in-house team.

## Named examples
- Weebly: took about 18 months to see PMF; tracked signups rather than active users, which is why it took so long; early NPS "80%"; free until Weebly Pro at four dollars a month; sold to Square for $365 million.
- iPhone: looked like a smartphone but created a new market of users.
- Virgin America: four reasons people choose airlines (schedule, price, destination, frequent flyer programs) were unwinnable, so it built a brand and product around the flying experience.
- Airbnb and Uber: markets that people first found weird ("I don't want to get in someone else's car").
- Steve Jobs: listens to problems and iterates; does not take solutions from customers.
- A South African competitor with $40 million that cloned Weebly's interface two weeks before an update.

## Tensions
- Contradicts "market research first" for new markets; says it only covers existing markets.
- Weebly went horizontal against the common advice to target a vertical; the speaker says verticals were not deep enough to support the product breadth required, then verticalized later.
- Suggests ignoring conversion rate and signups early, against common funnel dashboards.
- Says "don't overthink it, anecdotal is okay" yet warns against over-reading small-sample metrics.
- Says to stay small and micromanage until PMF, then scale aggressively; contradicts "delegate early".
- Speaker admits to not measuring KPIs for 18 months: "it probably could have been a lot shorter".
