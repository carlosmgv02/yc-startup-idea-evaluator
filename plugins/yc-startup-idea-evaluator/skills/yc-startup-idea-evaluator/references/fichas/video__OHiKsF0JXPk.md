## One-line thesis
Before product-market fit the goal is not a growth graph but a count of real users who love the product (1, then 10, 50, 100), and optimizing a metric or the investor pitch instead of user love is a self-defeating mistake.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Graphs are useless at near-zero users**: Quote: "graphs are a waste of time."
- **Count real users**: Quote: "do you have one real user with no asterisks?"
- **Goal ladder**: 1, 10, 50, 100 users who love it, then graphs. Quote: "if you could get 100 users or customers that love you, that is an awesome goal, and that's when I would start to graduate to graphs."
- **Churned users are zero users**: Quote: "So, you're what you're saying is you have zero users."
- **Love is the unit**: Quote: "Just get something that someone loves."
- **100 lovers imply growth**: Quote: "It's almost impossible in my experience to have something that a hundred people love that's like somehow not growing."
- **Lying about love or not looking at growth**: Quote: "You're either lying about the love or you're not looking at the growing."
- **7% a week is a result, not a cause**: Quote: "growing 7% week over week is a result of doing a lot of things right."
- **Founders mix cause and effect**: Quote: "they think it is the cause of the company working."
- **7% at small scale is easy, but not with churn**: Quote: "it's a lot harder to grow 7% week over week if you're churning all your users every week."
- **Cheating metrics (Goodhart)**: Quote: "they choose metrics that cheat."
- **Cumulative sign-ups instead of active use**: Quote: "We're going to use cumulative users that ever signed up and we're not going to worry about if they're using the product."
- **Time-in-product consistency check**: Ask how much time users must spend to get value and how much they actually spend. Quote: "to get value out of your product in a week, let's say, how much time should your users be spending in the product?"
- **Answers that disqualify**: "We're not measuring" or an actual time far below the claimed need. Quote: "One, we're not measuring."
- **Do they get value**: Quote: "do you think that your product is helping them in any way in their business, in their like in their goals?"
- **Post-PMF tooling does not transfer**: Quote: "the tactics and skills that you have working at a post-PMF company have no relation"
- **Build for yourself, add users slowly**: Gmail model. Quote: "he built it for himself and he was the first user."
- **Care over speed**: Quote: "someone that took the time and the care to make something that a small number of people loved and and were willing to run their life on."
- **Do not build for investors**: Quote: "you're building something for investors and not for users."
- **Seed odds**: Quote: "2% chance?"
- **Time on customers beats time on pitch**: Quote: "What if instead you spent that same amount of time actually interacting with your customers?"
- **Stripe delayed launch**: Quote: "they didn't launch for something like two years."
- **Customer obsession**: Quote: "They were that customer obsessed."
- **Test assumptions against famous companies**: Quote: "take whatever assumptions you have about early-stage startups and test them against your top 10 favorite companies."
- **Not the race to $100 million**: Quote: "That's not the race, guys."
- **Quantify the customer benefit**: Quote: "can you quantify the actual benefit of the customer as opposed to just time with you or that kind of stuff."
- **Define love so as not to cheat**: Quote: "how do we define love? Especially early, because like, so we don't cheat it."

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- How many real users do you have, with no asterisks, and how many of them love it? — three users who all churned.
- To get value in a week how much time should a user spend, and how much do they spend? — "we're not measuring" or "an hour a week" against a claimed hour a day.
- Are your users getting value? — "I don't know".
- What metric are you tracking and could you be gaming it? — cumulative sign-ups instead of active use.
- What are you building for: investors or users? — the plan is built around the raise.
- Can you put a dollar amount on the benefit to a customer? — only time in app.
- How long did your favorite companies take to reach 100 users? — assumptions never checked.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Showing growth graphs with a handful of users — "never show me these graphs again."
- Metric doctored to hit the goal — "all you're doing is screwing yourself."
- Treating the investor as the customer — "nothing good will come of that."
- Unmeasured engagement while claiming a core workflow.
- Culture that puts investors first until money is raised — "habit-forming in a negative way".
- Obsession with being fastest to $100 million.
- Using analytics-lab playbooks (Meta style A/B tests on tiny samples) with no users.

## Green flags
- Small number of users who "ran their life off" the product.
- Founder is the first user and adds users one or two a week (Gmail).
- Founder talks with users daily and can quantify their benefit.
- Users earn money or restaurants gain a third of revenue (clear benefit).
- Willingness to delay launch to build something that works (Stripe).

## Named examples
- Gmail, Paul Buchheit: built for himself, one or two added per week, under 100 users for months, hundreds of people who loved it by beta.
- Stripe: about two years before public launch; invite-only; Collison installation; Patrick Collison messaging early customers daily.
- Whatnot: marketplace where GMV is the benefit; sellers earn "a crazy living".
- DoorDash: a third of a restaurant's revenue.
- Twitch: streamers could quit their jobs.
- Meta and Google alumni: analytics habits do not apply pre-PMF.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the common YC heuristic of 7% weekly growth as the goal; the speakers reframe it as an outcome that comes from love and a way to expose churn, and grant "the genius of that advice".
- Nuance: "growing 7% week over week is great or horrible depending on how you do it."
- Contradicts "move fast, hockey stick graph" and "launch quickly" by citing Stripe and Gmail.
- Says investors love graphs yet advises not to optimize for them; the speaker concedes you can win a seed round that way, with "2% chance" of ultimate success.
- Warns against taking pop knowledge; yet says nothing is mandatory: "that doesn't mean you have to do it the same way".
