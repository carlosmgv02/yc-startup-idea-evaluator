## One-line thesis
Build the thing that annoys you, be user number one, watch for strong emotional reactions as the PMF signal, and protect your own joy and focus (fun is velocity) because viral scale brings security pressure, feature bloat and platform-dependency risk.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Annoyance as idea source**: Quote: "my source of inspiration is usually being annoyed"
- **User number one is you**: Quote: "User number one should be you And my users two to 20 were friends"
- **Strong emotion as PMF signal**: Quote: "each time there was a strong emotion and the best part there were quite a few people that really wanted it"
- **Non-technical people asking for access**: Quote: "especially to my nontechnical friends I told them no this is not yet for you and they got mad"
- **Attention is the scarce currency**: Quote: "eyeballs are kind of the the most expensive currency because building something so fast now"
- **Brand over product**: Quote: "Everything that everything you can build can be forked or cloned but your name cannot"
- **Distribution is the hardest problem**: Quote: "your hardest problem is not the tech it's not the software not even the people The hardest problem now is like getting eyeballs"
- **Hard and boring is easier to monetize**: Quote: "Maybe I would pick something again that's that's in the category hard and boring"
- **Dependency risk**: Quote: "Your dependencies business model is your business model"
- **Single-model optimization risk**: Quote: "the fact that I optimized too much on their model"
- **Feature cost comes after shipping**: Quote: "A new feature is is just a prompt away The real cost comes after"
- **Config option explosion**: Quote: "we ended up with around nine and a half thousand configuration options"
- **Maintenance asymmetry**: Quote: "It is infinitely harder to evolve software that has users"
- **Say no to popular PRs; write a vision file**: Quote: "I write a vision.mmd file where I kind of explain what it is now where I see this to be"
- **Correction travels slower than the scare**: Quote: "a correction never travels as far as a scare"
- **Agents can verify their own work**: Quote: "did I not give my agent everything they need to verify their work"
- **Code review as risk management**: Quote: "I see code review more as a as risk management"
- **Compute management is the agent-infrastructure bottleneck**: Quote: "there's no really good system of like managing all of that"
- **Proactive agents are a token-cost problem**: Quote: "that's not so much a tech problem It's more a token problem"
- **Fun predicts quality**: Quote: "Fun is velocity The weeks I enjoyed building the product got visibly better the weeks I didn't"
- **Do not stay in a business you do not love**: Quote: "I love building Programming was just a means to an end"
- **Differentiate from lab-sold agents**: Quote: "Every lab will sell you an agent Open claw is the alternative"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Who is user number one and are you it? — Red flag: founder does not use the product daily.
- What emotional reaction do early users show? — Red flag: polite interest only.
- Which single vendor or model could cut you off with 24 hours' notice? — Red flag: harness optimized for one provider's subscription.
- How many configuration options and users must you support, and who maintains them? — Red flag: feature count grows with no deprecation and no maintainers.
- What is your plan if you go viral (security reports, press, support)? — Red flag: one person is the whole response.
- What do you say no to? — Red flag: no written vision.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Dependence on a single provider's pricing or subscription terms (Anthropic cut with 24 hours' notice, he says).
- Founder stops using their own product and builds "for everyone".
- Hardening ignored by users: "Most of the users didn't care shit about that".
- Security-report spam with unverified agent-generated findings.
- Fun problems: "If you pick something that is fun even if it's hard you're gonna have a very tough time".

## Green flags
- Product that makes non-technical people angry when excluded.
- Open-source contributor flow: 18,000 people opened an issue or PR, almost 3,000 have commits (his figures).
- Rebound: weekly downloads from 835,000 (May low) to 4.7 million peak (his figures).

## Named examples
- OpenClaw (earlier Claudius/Clawdbot): WhatsApp relay turned viral Discord-hosted agent; foundation and nonprofit.
- Hermes agent: competitor that "beat us where it hurt".
- Anthropic: subscription disabled with about 24 hours' notice; OpenAI: tokens and employment; Nvidia: security help.
- His first company: B2B PDF framework business grown to almost 80 people, then burnout.

## Tensions
- Contradicts "move fast, add features": feature-led growth nearly sank the project.
- Contradicts "ignore hype": "A hype is like the weather".
- Personal-brand advice (his own virality is a mixed blessing; it almost made him delete the project).
- Says he does not read all code, while OpenAI work does read all code: risk-based.
