## One-line thesis
The best startup ideas grow organically from your own life and from what you wish someone would make for you, rather than being decided from afar to be needed by other users.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Wish-someone-would-make-it test**: Quote: "The best way to come up with startup ideas is to ask yourself the question: what do you wish someone would make for you?"
- **Organic vs made-up**: classify the idea; the most successful startups lean organic. Quote: "the most successful startups seem to be closer to the Apple type than the Viaweb type."
- **Founder as user**: check whether founders would use it themselves; if not, expect weaker user understanding. Quote: "I'd encourage anyone starting a startup to become one of its users, however unnatural it seems."
- **Young founders with made-up ideas**: Quote: "The worst ideas we see at Y Combinator are from young founders making things they think other people will want."
- **Experience needed to predict others**: Quote: "It takes experience to predict what other people will want."
- **Can this founder build it?**: Woz could design the computer he wanted. Quote: "Unlike most people who wanted computers, he could design one, so he did."
- **Obvious brokenness**: look for what is broken in daily life; you may need to stand outside yourself to see it. Quote: "You may need to stand outside yourself a bit to see brokenness, because you tend to get used to it and take it for granted."
- **Does not look like a startup**: organic ideas usually do not seem like startup ideas at first. Quote: "organic startup ideas usually don't seem like startup ideas at first."
- **Idea over startup**: fix broken things even if the problem seems too small to found a company on. Quote: "Just fix things that seem broken, regardless of whether it seems like the problem is important enough to build a company on."
- **Toy signal**: being dismissed as a toy is positive if users like it. Quote: "when someone comes to us with something that users like but that we could envision forum trolls dismissing as a toy, it makes us especially likely to invest."
- **Unmet need becoming fixable**: Quote: "There's nothing more valuable than an unmet need that is just becoming fixable."
- **Early adopters of tech as discoverers**: young founders use the latest tech so find fixable brokenness first. Quote: "because they use the latest stuff, they're in a position to discover valuable types of fixable brokenness first."
- **Weak spots of incumbents**: Quote: "things Steve Jobs doesn't use."
- **Open source exception**: Quote: "It's hard to compete directly with open source software."

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What do you wish someone would make for you, and did you build it? — founder is not the user and decided the need from afar.
- What was broken in your own life or work that led you here? — no personal origin.
- Why would users need this, and how do you know without being one of them? — young founder guessing at others' wants.
- Why has nobody fixed this already, and what just became fixable? — no reason the need is now addressable.
- (Open-source-adjacent) What part can you charge for? — nothing.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Idea decided "from afar" for a class of users other than the founders, especially from young founders.
- Founder moved into a "serious" startup idea instead of fixing a real itch (temptation to do something more "serious").
- Does not use the product and never becomes one of its users.
- Direct competition with open source without a chargeable piece.

## Green flags
- Founders themselves are the target user.
- Idea that looks like a toy or a side project but users like it.
- Founder has the skill to build the thing they want.
- Need that is unmet and just becoming fixable.

## Named examples
- Apple (Wozniak): built the computer he wanted; iPhone is the phone Jobs wants.
- Viaweb: made-up type; Graham says they should have become direct marketers and opened their own store.
- Microsoft (Gates): wrote the Basic interpreter he would use on the Altair.
- Google: Larry and Sergey wrote what they would use.
- Facebook: began as a project, not a startup; Harvard printed facebook was obviously broken.

## Tensions
- Contrasts with the common advice to find a big market first: "focus more on the idea part and less on the startup part."
- Graham admits Viaweb (made-up type) succeeded, "no sharp line between the two types".
- Young founders are worst at made-up ideas but best source of organic ones.
