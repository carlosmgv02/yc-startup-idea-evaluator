## One-line thesis
The first 10 customers come from the founder's network and from manual, in-person or in-community effort where the buyer actually lives, not from outreach tools; tools matter only after 10 to 20 quality customers.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Channel follows buyer**: the founder must be able to say where the buyer spends time before choosing a channel. Quote: "where does your target customer actually spend their time"
- **Buyer-day test**: if the founder cannot answer concrete questions about the buyer's day, they have not talked to enough customers. Quote: "if you can't answer most of these questions concretely yet that's a sign you haven't spent enough time with real customers"
- **Wrong-channel false negative**: cold email failing for non-desk buyers (school administrators, property managers, truck dispatchers) does not mean outbound failed. Quote: "you can blast them with cold email all you want and you're just going to conclude that outbound sales doesn't work for you"
- **Trade-show proof**: one founder "closed more in 3 days than he had in 3 months of sending cold emails". Quote: "he closed more in 3 days than he had in 3 months of sending cold emails"
- **Customers 1 to 3 are warm**: no counter-examples found in the founder survey; if a founder's first three are strangers, ask why. Quote: "customers 1 through three almost always come from your personal network"
- **Early buyers buy the founder**: trust in the builder drives the first bets. Quote: "the people buying your product early are buying because they trust you as a founder not just because of the quality of the product"
- **Network before tools**: unmessaged second-degree connections are lowest hanging fruit. Quote: "tons of founders spend weeks setting up outreach tools when they actually have a lot of secondderee connections they haven't messaged yet"
- **Warm intro quality**: specificity about who, why they care and what to forward. Quote: "the easier you make it for them the more likely the intro actually happens"
- **Presence over Zoom**: early closes come from being in the room, despite cost and rejection. Quote: "it also happens to work better than basically anything else"
- **Persistence signal**: a founder flew to one executive buyer four weeks in a row, another flew to Hawaii, was kicked out after 8 minutes and got a top account. Quote: "that customer through some persistence became one of his biggest accounts"
- **Conference calendar playbook**: back-to-back 15-minute Calendly slots before a small industry conference, emailing the attendee list. Quote: "put 15-minute slots back to back across every day of the conference before the event starts"
- **Micro dinners**: 6 to 10 ICP people, $50 to $100 each, convert better than big events. Quote: "once someone's eating dinner with you it's very unlikely they're going to ignore your email afterwards"
- **Find public complaints**: look for where the pain is expressed publicly (Reddit, Facebook groups, forums) and DM individually. Quote: "find the place where the pain is being expressed in public"
- **Lurkers exist**: a hostile Reddit thread can still convert silent readers. Quote: "a couple hundred lurkers the people who never would comment who might quietly sign up for their product"
- **Non-sales framing (only if genuine)**: advice, mentorship, product review, whiteboard session. Quote: "if you genuinely want to learn and invest in building a relationship with these people this framing can often be a real foot in the door"
- **Research at scale before a product**: one founder talked to 200 salespeople before building; 50% accepted connection requests, 20% converted to calls. Quote: "by the time she had a product she already had a pipeline of people who told her exactly what they needed"
- **Pay for feedback in high-ACV markets**: lawyers paid $100 to $200 per hour; about 30% accepted and CAC stayed reasonable. Quote: "the conversion rate was high enough that his customer acquisition cost ended up being quite reasonable"
- **Give before asking**: a vulnerability scan, a product walk-through, a compliance audit note prepared for the prospect. Quote: "20 minutes of work before asking for 30 minutes of someone's time is actually quite a reasonable trade"
- **Copy rules**: under 75 words, one clear call to action, read aloud test. Quote: "keep it under 75 words long emails tend to just get ignored"
- **Phase model**: 1 to 3 network, 4 to 10 unscalable, 10 to 50 start tools. Quote: "customers 10 through 50 are where the playbook starts to shift"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Who were your first three customers and how did you reach them? — Cold channels, or none yet and no network use.
- Where does your buyer spend their day, and which channel did you pick because of it? — Default to email and LinkedIn because it is easy.
- When did you last meet a buyer face to face? — Never.
- Which public threads show people complaining about this problem, and how many have you DM'd? — Has not looked.
- What do you give a prospect before asking for 30 minutes? — Nothing.
- How many conversations happened before building? — None.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Weeks of tooling and subject-line tuning before working the existing network.
- Concluding "outbound does not work" from low email reply rates with a non-desk buyer.
- Automating before 10 to 20 quality customers.
- Never being in the same room as the buyer.
- Cannot describe the buyer's day.

## Green flags
- Founder-led, manual, personalized outreach in the first 10.
- Warm intros producing around half of a batch's closes in one founder's account.
- Founder travels repeatedly for one buyer.
- Prospects given specific, researched value up front.

## Named examples
- Trade-show founder: 3 days beat 3 months of cold email.
- Salesperson-researcher: 200 conversations pre-product.
- Lawyer-feedback founder: paid per hour for feedback, about 30% acceptance.
- Healthcare founder: 2 to 5 Reddit and Facebook replies per day for months.
- Tools named: Apollo, Clay, LinkedIn Premium, Happenstance.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the common scalable-first reflex (automation, sequences) and the "cold email at volume" habit.
- Adds a caveat to "do things that don't scale": the speaker says not to disguise a sales call as mentorship if the founder is not open to learning.
- Evidence is a compilation of Bookface answers, so it is anecdotal and survivor-biased.
