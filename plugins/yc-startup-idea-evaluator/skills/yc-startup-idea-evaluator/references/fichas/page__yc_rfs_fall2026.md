## One-line thesis
Quote: "AI is moving into the physical world."  YC wants founders rebuilding the systems that power the real world (education, healthcare, defense, finance, infrastructure, work), plus AI-native software categories where agents change the shape of the product.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Platform shift mints consumer giants**: a new platform needs a visible cost/capability curve to time entry. Quote: "Follow the curve, and you can predict the consumer moment: it lands very soon."
- **Price falling 10x a year**: build today's expensive magic and let token costs fall into viability. Quote: "today, the magic can run $1,000 a month in tokens for each user, but that is falling 10x a year"
- **Unit economics of labor vs software**: sell where the buyer spends far more on labor than on software. Quote: "these industries spend 10 to 100x more on labor than software"
- **Own the data the incumbents cannot see**: recording work end to end gives a data moat vs labs and incumbents. Quote: "You'll record all the work as it actually happens."
- **Incumbents charge for the old model**: if incumbents bill for human coordination and visibility, an OS managing robot and human labor is a different category. Quote: "The incumbents charge for human coordination and visibility."
- **Do not just automate the old process**: best compliance product rethinks operations with AI as default. Quote: "The best version of this doesn't just automate existing processes"
- **AI-native problem test**: work that is monitoring, flagging, reporting, audit trails is cheaper by AI; if the solution still has a human-review bottleneck it is exposed. Quote: "These are tasks that AI can handle faster and cheaper than humans."
- **Willingness of buyers to grant access**: agentic coding tools showed enterprises will give codebase access if valuable. Quote: "willing to give codebase access to external tools, provided they're valuable"
- **Single-player to multiplayer**: tools that win turn solo into team use. Quote: "AI hasn't had its multiplayer moment yet."
- **Trust layer rebuilt**: trust signals built when faking was expensive no longer hold. Quote: "Every trust signal we have was built for a world where faking a human was expensive, and that world is gone."
- **Counter-cyclical timing**: bear markets favor builders in crypto. Quote: "bear markets let the real projects build and thrive while bull markets are the worst time to build"
- **Customer profile for demand**: large demographic shift with almost no purpose-built tech. Quote: "almost no technology is actually built for older people"
- **Supplement, not replace**: the Primer is pitched as a parent-bought entry point, not a teacher replacement. Quote: "Not a replacement for teachers, but a supplement that makes them more effective."

## The requests (partner, one-line thesis)
- **The Primer** (Andrew Miklas): AI tutor for young children. Quote: "a product that adaptively teaches young children to read, write, and do arithmetic, at the quality of a devoted private tutor and at consumer scale"
- **The Future of American Defense** (Daniel P. Driscoll, US Secretary of the Army): low-cost interceptors and open-architecture components. Quote: "We are actively funding low-cost interceptors or any component that helps us lower the cost per kill."
- **A Cloud for Small Software** (Pete Koomen): a cloud for agent-built tools with one or few users. Quote: "Small software should be as easy to share with your colleagues as a Google Doc."
- **Multiplayer AI** (Aaron Epstein): shared live agent sessions for teams. Quote: "if you're building AI that's multiplayer by default, we'd love to hear from you"
- **Compute at Sea** (Francois Chaubard): offshore modular compute vessels. Quote: "We want to fund founders who are bringing the world's compute to the oceans."
- **AI-Powered Consumer Products for 1 Billion People** (Raphael Schaad): consumer apps now that agents act like people. Quote: "CONSUMER is going to be so back."
- **AI for the Aging Population** (Max Kolysh): voice, monitoring, robotics and caregiver software for seniors. Quote: "By 2030, one in five Americans will be over 65, and there's nowhere near enough people to take care of everyone."
- **New Operating Systems for the Physical World** (Charlie Warren): software managing agents, robots and humans in construction, maintenance and fleet operations. Quote: "80% of the global workforce doesn't sit at a desk."
- **The Best Time to Build in Crypto** (Nemil Dalal): stablecoins, agentic commerce, tokenized assets, ramps. Quote: "Eventually, we expect every YC startup to use crypto rails from capital raising to payments, though most will probably never even know."
- **Data for the Real World** (Austin Tindle and Diana Hu): new ways to collect dense physical-world data. Quote: "once you can model a system, you can control it."
- **Proving You're Human** (Max Kolysh): verified-human trust layer against deepfake fraud. Quote: "rebuilding the trust layer of the internet: knowing there's a verified human on the other end of a call, a message, a transaction"
- **AI-Native Compliance Infrastructure** (Daivik Goel): consolidated financial compliance with AI as default. Quote: "the cost of staying compliant grows faster than revenue"
- **Self-Maintaining APIs** (Harsha Gaddipati): agents that apply API provider changes to customer codebases. Quote: "API providers shouldn't just announce changes; they should apply them."

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What changed in the last 12 months that makes this possible now? — "Nothing, it was always possible."
- Does your cost per user fall with model prices, and what is the margin at today's price? — Only viable if tokens stay expensive or free.
- If an incumbent just adds an agent, what remains? — Only a UI over the same human-review workflow.
- What data does your product capture that labs and incumbents never see? — None; no proprietary data loop.
- Are you replacing a process or rethinking it with AI as default? — Faithful port of the manual process.
- Will customers actually grant the access your product needs (codebase, records)? — No evidence of willingness.
- Who is the single buyer and what labor spend does it displace? — Pitched as software spend only.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Single-player AI tool with no path to team use (Epstein).
- Compliance or workflow tool keeping manual review bottlenecks (Goel).
- Solution depends on a trust signal that deepfakes can fake (Kolysh).
- Product for the physical world with no field data advantage (Warren, Tindle/Hu).
- Entering crypto because prices are high rather than building (Dalal).

## Green flags
- Massive underserved demographic with unfilled labor (aging, 53 million unpaid family caregivers).
- Spend ratio of 10-100x labor vs software.
- Founders already working with the target vendors (Gaddipati: 50+ API vendors).
- Concrete early companies cited: Gecko Robotics, Sorcerer, Hyperliquid, BlindPay, Infinia, Aspora.
- A specific hook for timing (cost curve, regulation clarity, agent adoption).

## Named examples
- Gecko Robotics, Sorcerer: physical-world data collection.
- Hyperliquid: tiny team challenging exchanges.
- Deel, Gusto, Stripe, Coinbase, Axiom: YC fintechs building on or with crypto.
- BlindPay, Infinia (LatAm ramps), Aspora (money to India).
- Claude Code, Devin, Greptile: proof enterprises grant codebase access.
- Google Docs vs Word, Figma vs Photoshop: multiplayer wins.

## Tensions
- Partners ask for broad "AI-native" rethinking while earlier YC sales talks (Blomfield, Koomen) push a narrow wedge first.
- Crypto thesis states the bear market is the best time, against typical timing advice.
- Consumer request admits the unit cost ($1,000 per user per month) is unviable today.
- Physical-world OS and data requests lean on the same labor-spend logic as the FDE model (McGrew).
