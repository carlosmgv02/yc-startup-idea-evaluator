## One-line thesis
By analogy with the SaaS boom, vertical AI agents (software plus the people's work in one product) can produce hundreds of unicorns and be about 10x bigger than the SaaS company each one displaces, because they attack payroll spend and not just software spend.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Category-size claim**: the bet is a category of $300 billion plus companies. Quote: "there are going to be $300 billion plus companies started just in this one category"
- **SaaS history baseline**: Quote: "over 40% of all venture capital dollars in that time period went to SAS companies and we produced over 300 SAS unicorns in that 20-year time period"
- **Enabling technology first**: new paradigm makes new things possible; ask what the enabler is. Quote: "it's a new Computing Paradigm that makes it possible to just like do something fundamentally different"
- **Obvious consumer ideas go to incumbents**: Quote: "zero startups won in those categories 100% of the value flow to incumbents"
- **Non-obvious ideas incumbents avoid because of risk**: Quote: "why would you endanger that pot of gold to sort of pursue these things that uh might be scary or might ruin the pot of gold"
- **Incumbents will not do deep vertical**: Quote: "there's no one to Google who really understands payroll and has the patience to like deal with all the nuances of all these like stupid payroll regulations"
- **Vertical depth moat**: each B2B product requires deep domain focus. Quote: "each B2B SAS company really requires like the people who are running the product in the business to be extremely deep in one domain and care very deeply about a"
- **Broad platforms give worse UX**: Quote: "they have to cover so much ground the user experience is actually pretty bad"
- **Software plus people**: Quote: "the vertical AI equivalent is just going to be the software plus the people in one product"
- **Payroll spend bigger than software spend**: Quote: "they spend way more on employees than they do on software"
- **10x claim**: Quote: "it's very possible the vertical equivalence will be 10 times as large as the SAS company that they are disrupting"
- **Selling to the team that gets replaced**: sell to a decision maker high enough that the team cannot sabotage. Quote: "if you're going to go and sell to the team that's going to get replaced by AI they're going to sabotage it"
- **Remove the buyer friction**: replacing instead of assisting changes who you sell to (Rainforest QA vs Mtic [sic]). Quote: "their pitch is not oh this like makes your QA people faster it's like this just means you don't need a QA team at all"
- **Sell to companies without the team yet**: Quote: "you can go and sell to companies that don't even have big QA teams at the moment"
- **Crowded category can be empty at the high end**: AI customer support has "a 100" shallow entrants. Quote: "almost all of those companies are doing very simple like zero shot llm prompting that can't actually replace a real customer support team"
- **Real depth test**: Quote: "to actually replace a customer support team for like an at Scale company that has like 100 customer support reps that do lots of complicated things every day you need"
- **Market penetration gap**: Quote: "there were only like three or four companies that were even attempting to do that and cumulative they had cumulatively they had like less than 1% Market penetration"
- **Specificity moat via evals**: Quote: "it's 10,000 test cases in a very detailed uh eval set that you know is basically just for zepto and things like zepto"
- **Tailored solutions prevent a meta-platform**: Quote: "the customers just require really heavily like tailored Solutions and it's hard to build one that like works for every everyone"
- **Wrapper risk**: first wave LLM apps got crushed. Quote: "that first wave of llm apps mostly did get crushed by the next wave of GPT"
- **Voice thresholds**: timing windows open when quality and latency cross a line. Quote: "even if you went 6 months back it felt like the voices were not realistic enough yet the latency was too high"
- **Retention risk on thin platforms**: Quote: "how do you hold on to them especially as you uh run into things like the new open AI voice apis"
- **Where to look**: Quote: "you got to find some boring repeative admin work somewhere"
- **Boring admin means billion-dollar potential**: Quote: "if you can find a boring repetitive admin task um there is likely going to be a billion dollar AI agent startup if you keep digging deep enough into it"
- **Founder/near-founder access to the work**: Quote: "one of the founders mother is a dentist and so he just decided to go to work with her for a day and just sit there seeing what she did"
- **Classic robotics maxim adapted**: Quote: "look for boring butter passing jobs"
- **Enterprise buyers have learned point solutions**: Quote: "Enterprises have already been trained on like the value of Point Solutions and vertical Solutions"
- **Small teams with leverage**: Quote: "a future unicorn company that's only run if we take it to the limit with only 10 employees that's completely plausible"
- **Engineers as leverage**: Quote: "if you put a really smart engineer on some of these like tasks they just find ways to make they find leverage"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Which function or team does your agent replace, and what does that team cost the buyer? — red flag: pitch is only "makes people faster", software-budget sizing only
- Who signs, and will the team being replaced block it? — red flag: selling bottom-up to the team that gets automated
- What does your agent do that a zero-shot prompt cannot? — red flag: demo-grade workflow, no evals
- How many test cases and how specific is your eval set to a customer? — red flag: general purpose, no customer-specific evals
- What do you do when the foundation model or its voice API ships your feature? — red flag: ChatGPT-wrapper with no depth
- How did you find this admin task? — red flag: chosen from the top down, no first-hand exposure
- Why would a big incumbent not build this? — red flag: no answer; or the space is one where incumbents "win all of those" (general assistants, search)
- Is the boring work specific enough to be a vertical, and does the incumbent have the domain depth? — red flag: broad horizontal assistant

## Red flags / kill signals
- General-purpose consumer assistant or search — "probably the incumbents will win all of those"
- Thin wrapper over a model — got crushed by the next GPT release
- Simple prompting presented as replacing a support team — "just kind of makes for like a nice demo"
- Selling to the team the product replaces — they will sabotage
- Voice or API platform with no plan to hold customers — "how do you hold on to them"
- Idea not tied to a repetitive admin task

## Green flags
- Boring, repetitive admin work with large headcount (collections calls, claims processing, government bid scanning, QA testing, technical screens)
- Deep, customer-specific eval sets (10,000 test cases)
- Top-down sale to a decision maker who benefits from the replacement
- Founder with direct experience or access to the work (friend refreshing a government site; mother a dentist)
- Rapid traction in Enterprises (faster than seen before, per the speakers)

## Named examples
- Salesforce: first true SaaS company; believed impossible at first
- Viaweb (PG): first SaaS app, hampered by missing XMLHttpRequest
- Google Docs: one of about 30 companies trying to bring Office online
- Uber, Airbnb: incumbents did not clone due to regulatory risk
- Gusto: payroll depth incumbents lack
- Outset (surveys vs Qualtrics), Mtic [sic] (QA vs Rainforest QA), Apriora [sic, "a priora"] (technical screen), cap.ai [sic] (dev support), Powerhelp [sic, "Power help"/"parel"] (customer support), Giga ML [sic, "gig ml"] for Zepto (30,000 tickets a day), Salient (auto-loan collection voice calls), Sweet spot [sic] (government bids), Vapi [sic] (voice infra), Vector Shift (enterprise agent platform), Sierra (Brett Taylor)
- Rippling (Parker Conrad): counter-example of horizontal bundling
- Speedy Brand: early LLM wrapper that wrote blogs

## Tensions
- Contradicts "software is the market": claim is that AI agents address labor, not software budget.
- Verticalization versus horizontal bundling: Rippling is "the case against vertical" and each new product "hits like multi-millions of dollars in ARR on day one".
- Early winners might be general-purpose platforms ("make it easy for you to do llm stuff") before vertical agents; the speakers lean toward vertical from day one.
- Customer support is "famously crowded" yet considered open for depth; contradicts "crowded means avoid".
- Open question whether LLMs go beyond summarizing to "actual thinking" and replace management.
