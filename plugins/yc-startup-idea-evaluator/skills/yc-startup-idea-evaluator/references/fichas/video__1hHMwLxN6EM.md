## One-line thesis
An MVP is something ridiculously simple given to the first target users to see if you deliver any value at all; launch something bad quickly, get any customers, talk to them, iterate on the solution while holding the problem and the customer tightly.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **MVP definition**: Quote: "This is the first thing you can give to the very first set of users you want to target in order to see if you can deliver any value at all to them."
- **Know a real user with the problem**: first users come from the problem you chose. Quote: "theoretically you decide to solve a problem that you know someone has. So the way you get your first user is you talk to that person that you know has the problem."
- **Mysterious user set**: Quote: "if you are building a product for a mysterious set of users that you have no idea who they are, question that slightly."
- **Own user advantage**: Quote: "It's even more helpful if you're your own user so you can tell whether your product's working for you."
- **Launch quickly**: Quote: "launch something bad quickly."
- **Get any customers**: Quote: "You'd be surprised at how many founders journeys end before a single user has actually interacted with a product they've created."
- **Feedback on the small thing is not useless**: Quote: "hold the problem you're solving tightly, hold the customer tightly, hold the solution you're building loosely."
- **Iterate vs pivot (screwdriver)**: keep the user and problem, fix the solution instead of hunting for new problems for your tool. Quote: "keep the mechanic. Keep the problem. I need to screw something in."
- **Weeks, not months**: Quote: "you should be able to build it fast in weeks, not months."
- **Landing page and spreadsheet**: Quote: "we see startups just start with a landing page and a spreadsheet."
- **Narrow scope**: focus on a small initial set of users and their highest-order problem. Quote: "they should just focus on a small set of initial users and their highest order problems and then ignore the rest until later."
- **Heavy MVP for regulated or hard tech**: Quote: "your MVP can start with a simple simple website that explains what you do."
- **Redefine launch**: Quote: "launch is when you get any customers."
- **Research vs product in front of people**: Quote: "until you can put something in front of people"
- **Pitch deck vs product**: Quote: "spending all that time on a pitch deck is not as valuable as spending your time building anything that you can give to a customer."
- **Time-box the spec**: Quote: "the only things that could be on my spec are things I can build in three weeks."
- **Write the spec down**: Quote: "your three-week plan turns into a three-month plan."
- **Cut the spec**: Quote: "if there's no non-important things, start cutting important things."
- **Do not fall in love with the MVP**: Quote: "none of the products I showed you before was the initial vision, what it ended up being."

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Who is your first user, by name, and have you talked to them? — "mysterious set of users".
- What can you give them in three weeks? — plan measured in months, long feature list.
- Have any real people used it yet? — no single user has interacted with it.
- What did users say, and what did you change in the solution (not the problem)? — no iteration, or jumped to other problems for the same tool.
- Why are you not launching yet? — waiting for a press launch or the full vision.
- (Regulated or hard tech) What is your heavy MVP, and what can you show in days? — nothing to refer to.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Journey ends before a single user touches the product.
- Feedback dismissed as useless because the product is not the "full thing".
- Falling in love with the solution and searching for other problems it might solve (the screwdriver that cooks).
- Spec never written down, scope drifts after each user or investor comment.
- Magical press launch fantasy.
- Pitch deck work instead of building.

## Green flags
- Founder is their own user.
- First users are known people with the problem.
- Ugly, tiny v1 shipped in weeks.
- Small set of initial users with their top problem.

## Named examples
- Airbnb 2008: no payments, no map view, part-time coder; money exchanged in person.
- Twitch (Justin.tv): one channel, low-res video, no video games.
- Stripe (/dev/payments): no bank deals, almost no features, founders integrated it for users at their office.
- Google, Facebook, Twitter: nobody remembers their launch day.

## Tensions
- Redefining "launch" as getting any customers, not a press launch.
- Regulated or hard-tech startups need a "heavy MVP" and cannot ship in weeks.
- Says talk to some users before building but not a long research period.
