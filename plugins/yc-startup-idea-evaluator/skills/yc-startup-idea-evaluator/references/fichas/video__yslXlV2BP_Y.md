## One-line thesis
In 2026, YC data shows hard tech, full-end-to-end "do the job" agent products, data/RL-environment vendors and experienced or solo founders growing fastest; systems of record survive only if they become harnesses.

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Does your product do the whole job, or just track it? — Red flag: point solution needing a human operator.
- If you are a system of record, are you also the place where work happens? — Red flag: only an MCP/API over data that can be copied elsewhere.
- Would an agent pick your product? — Red flag: designed for humans clicking only.
- How fast can you reach revenue compared to the batch median (about $20,000 MRR by end of batch)? — Red flag: no revenue path in months.
- Is demand durable beyond AI hype budgets? — Red flag: revenue from experimentation budgets only.
- If hard tech: what in the software or model stack makes this possible with a small team now? — Red flag: needs large engineering headcount.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Point-solution SaaS where "you needed someone to operate the SaaS software".
- Systems of record that only expose an MCP and lose the data moat.
- Data/RL vendors with fragile customer concentration (the labs); the source notes stealth and "benchmark maxing" risk.
- Revenue explained by AI hype budgets.

## Green flags
- Agent does the end-to-end workflow (insurance broker, clinical intake, medical billing, recruiting outreach to interviews).
- Per-account revenue expansion as agents take on more (Juicebox: "double or triple", per the host).
- Customers that are fast-growing startups needing a fast supplier.
- Technical founders with PhDs (one in six in the current summer batch, per the host).

## Tensions
- Contradicts the "SaaS is dead" claim: SaaS stocks recovered, so moats may hold for now.
- Contradicts "co-founders are essential": more solo founders at start, but co-founders still add value and often join later.
