## One-line thesis
Encord survived a couple of years "in the desert" by betting on AI generalisation and the data layer, then rode the market arriving (ChatGPT, then physical AI); product-market fit felt like gradual compounding, not a moment, and founders should choose location by where customers, talent and investors are.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **PMF = product and market converging**: Product-market fit comes from a rising product plus a market that moves toward you; sometimes the world changes. Check which of the two is moving. Quote: "Sometimes it's the market comes to the product you know the world changes and suddenly the thing that people didn't care about now care about"
- **Gradual PMF**: No binary threshold; it can feel like "a little bit better every day". Do not wait for a single launch moment. Quote: "it just felt like it was a little bit better every day it just kind of was slowly slowly compounding"
- **Sale closed without you knowing the company**: One co-founder's PMF signal was seeing a sale close in the sales channel for a company they did not recognise. Quote: "when we saw you know a sale close in the Gong channel and we didn't know what the company did"
- **Belief persists through the desert**: Check whether the founders can articulate a long-horizon thesis that explains why they stay. Quote: "we were in the desert for a couple years"
- **Market opening event**: Early conviction was hard to sell until a macro event (ChatGPT) made buyers open to the conversation. Quote: "it was more they were open to the conversation"
- **Founder-led sales then hire by trial**: Sales hiring took three teams; the third worked. Quote: "we brought in a sales team it didn't work We brought another sales team it didn't work By the third sales team it started to work"
- **Fire fast**: A bad hire was kept nine months; the lesson is to follow your gut faster. Quote: "what we've gotten a lot better at over the years is just following your gut and making the right decision faster"
- **YC advice is almost always right**: Ignoring advice on a specific hire led to a mistake. Quote: "the advice of YC is always right Yeah No it's it's almost always right but you still make the mistake"
- **Start with the hardest modality**: Vision first as the hardest modality, expecting multimodal future; the market later moved to physical AI. Quote: "our thesis originally starting with vision was it was the hardest modality to start with"
- **Synthesise next bet from conversations**: Find the next bet by talking to customers, prospects and co-founders, not by imagining in a room. Quote: "it's not us sitting in a room quietly trying to imagine the future it's really being out there and getting as much data as we can"
- **Market size anchor**: "80% of economic activity is manipulating or moving things in the real world" (speaker's claim) and robotics folks expecting more robots than people in 5 to 10 years. Quote: "80% of economic activity is manipulating or moving things in the real world"
- **Location by where the customers are**: Be where the important people are: customers, then talent, then investors; deep tech may weight talent most. Quote: "you should just be where the important people for your company are So for us it's customers and then talent and then investors in that order"
- **Welcome strong competitors**: Competitors pushed the company; many pivoted, consolidated or ran out of money. Quote: "we wanted to be competitive We prefer to have really good competitors because they they push you They make you better as as a company"
- **Differentiate by scale**: Focus on physical AI and operating at multi-petabyte scale. Quote: "being able to operate on the multiple pedibyte level of data which is hard for people to do"
- **Customers arrive when internal tools hit the wall**: Buyers come after trying in-house tools and hitting the scaling inflection. Quote: "usually where people come to us is when they've built internal tools they've tried PC and they're at the inflection point where they need scale"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What is changing in the market that makes your product relevant now? — No outside shift; depends only on the product.
- How will you know you have PMF if it comes gradually? — Waiting for a launch spike.
- Who is the best-positioned customer: those whose internal tools have hit the wall? — Targeting the earliest, smallest prospects only.
- Why would you locate where you are? — Chosen by comfort rather than customers and talent.
- Who are your best competitors and what do they do better? — Claims no competition.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Hiring against advice and keeping a known bad hire — nine months lost.
- Sales team hired before founder-led sales is understood — two failed teams.
- Selling too early to customers who do not yet need the product at scale — the speaker declines to oversell to early-stage teams.

## Green flags
- Founder keeps selling and is "intensely involved in every single part" of early deals.
- Hundreds of customers, multiple petabytes of multimodal data (speaker's figures).
- Roller-coaster mindset: run at the fire.

## Named examples
- Encord: data curation, annotation and evaluation for physical AI (robotics, autonomous vehicles, logistics, manufacturing; also cow facial recognition).
- Competitors named by the host: Scale and Mercor.
- Gong channel: where the sale closed unseen.

## Tensions
- Quitting a quant job that earned more in a month than three prior years combined; the speaker says money was not the reason.
- Contradicts "find PMF with a clear before and after": the speaker says there was never a single moment.
- Location advice (customers, talent, investors in that order) differs from "move to SF" absolutism; the co-founder moved but the CEO stayed in London.
