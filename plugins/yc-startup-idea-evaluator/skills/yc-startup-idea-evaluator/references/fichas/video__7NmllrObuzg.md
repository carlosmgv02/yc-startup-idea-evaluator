## One-line thesis
Great consumer startups today come from finding the ultra-premium experience that rich people already get and making it 100x better and available to everyone with AI, not from replacing humans with chatbots that are 95% as good or making a 10% better version of an existing app.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Premium experience to the masses**: Check what the most premium version of the experience is and whether the startup delivers it cheaply to everyone. Quote: "look at what the most premium experiences in the world are that people with infinite resources have."
- **Technology as democratization**: Quote: "takes something that is seen as just super premium and way way too expensive to be practical"
- **Assume an ultra-premium version exists that you do not know**: Do not start from what you get today. Quote: "the trap might be to start from what you're getting and try to make it 10% better."
- **Go find it**: Quote: "assume there's an ultra premium version you don't know about. Go find it."
- **Ask rich people**: Quote: "maybe you just ask them how do how do they get a tutor? How do they travel?"
- **Head-of-state test**: Quote: "what kind of health experience does a head of state have?"
- **Star-rating stretch (Airbnb)**: Push the experience several levels above 5 stars. Quote: "what's the fivestar version not the fourstar okay what's the sixstar"
- **Invention not required**: The premium experience probably exists but does not scale. Quote: "you might not have to invent these experiences. They probably exist. It's probably just that they don't scale very well or they're very expensive"
- **100x better test**: The product must feel magical, not cheaper. Quote: "magical feeling that we felt with these 100x better products"
- **Save-money pitch is weak**: Replacing humans on cost or time grounds is not enough. Quote: "the pitch that the founders have is that it's saving money"
- **Do not approximate humans at 95%**: Quote: "approximating a 95% as good version of uh of what humans do"
- **Faceless automation is not premium**: Quote: "I am personally not convinced that tons of AI automation that remains faceless and unpersonalized is actually the move."
- **Infinite personalization**: Quote: "what AI allows is for infinite personalization."
- **Expert in me**: Quote: "they're an expert in me."
- **AI makes previously unworkable models work**: Quote: "AI makes the business that wouldn't work before AI work after"
- **Do not be stuck on failed predecessors**: Quote: "oh, well, there have been a bunch of different kayaks and none of them worked. It's like you're not getting it."
- **Ask what amazing would be**: Quote: "what is an 11star experience?"
- **Competition for attention is brutal today**: Quote: "you have to be way more compelling."
- **Joy over addiction**: Quote: "I probably can't make more addictive algorithms, but can I produce more joy?"
- **Remove maintenance**: Quote: "not only is is this service going to pick the things that it knows is good for me, it's going to take responsibility for whatever needs to be maintained"
- **Four Seasons test**: Quote: "What things can you take for granted?"
- **Compete with nothing (old advantage)**: Quote: "it is much easier to blow people away with the experience"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What is the most premium version of this experience that a rich person gets today, and have you experienced it? — founder has never seen the high end.
- Is your product 100x better or a bit cheaper or faster? — the pitch is cost or time savings.
- What would the 11-star version of this be? — no answer after years on the idea.
- Is it more personal than a human could be (does it know me)? — faceless chatbot or voice agent.
- What are you competing with for attention or time? — ignoring TikTok-scale incumbents.
- Why couldn't this work before AI? — it could, and many tried.
- What recurring maintenance or drudgery do you remove entirely? — only automates a single step such as searching.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Consumer AI that replaces a human with a chatbot or voice agent with "95%" quality and a cost-saving pitch.
- Making an existing product 10% better (a 10% better Kayak).
- Itinerary generation or flight-finding automation with no premium outcome — "Is a premium experience to hand you an itinerary and say, "No," [sic]".
- Anonymous, faceless AI interactions that make everyone you talk to dislike you (AI replies to email).
- Trying to out-addict TikTok or X with a more addictive algorithm.
- Founders with no definition of "amazing".

## Green flags
- Founder has experienced or researched the premium version (fixer, private banker, personal chef).
- Experience is personalized to the individual and responsible for the outcome.
- Product removes the whole chore (maintenance) rather than assisting one step.
- AI enables an economic model that did not previously work.
- Competing with nothing or a clearly bad status quo.

## Named examples
- Napster, Netflix streaming, Google Maps: easy to be 10x better when competing with nothing.
- Uber Black: took black cars, a premium experience, to anyone via a button.
- Airbnb, Brian Chesky: the 11-star experience brainstorm (parade and elephant).
- Kayak: blew away airline sites and travel agents; a chatbot version is not magic.
- Fixer (Anthony Bourdain, No Reservations): premium travel that knows the place and you.
- Private banker and fintech (Brex mentioned): the premium banking experience is a person who knows you; text them and it happens.
- Personalized education: one-size-fits-all school versus a tutor.
- Amazon (SEO'd to heck, "crap problem") and DoorDash (personal chef): premium versions of each.
- TikTok, Facebook, X: the attention black hole that consumer products now compete with.
- Twitch: "you can make content online and get paid" was the 11-star experience then.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the intuition that cost or time savings are the AI pitch; says the pitch should be 100x better.
- Nuances Christensen-style disruption (bottom-up cheap) with a top-down view: start from the premium and democratize it.
- Admits it is harder now: old founders "were competing with nothing", while today's bar is far higher.
- AI should add personalization, against the common view that AI means faceless automation and agents.
- One speaker notes "I don't know how I do that" about producing joy: the heuristic is not a recipe.
