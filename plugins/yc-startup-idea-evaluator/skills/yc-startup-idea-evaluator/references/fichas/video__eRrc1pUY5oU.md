## One-line thesis
Intelligence is a rented commodity while context, skills and memory are the owned asset; founders (and employees) should keep their "skill files" and brain in a repo they control, because agents now give one person the leverage of a team.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Leverage is in context, not weights**: Same model, 2x people vs 100x people; check where the founder's edge sits. Good: proprietary context and well-timed retrieval. Bad: "we use GPT". Quote: "the leverage is not in the weights it's in what context you give it how relevant it is and does it happen at the right step"
- **Model rented, context owned**: Test the defensibility equation: rented frontier model plus unique owned context plus a harness. Quote: "a frontier model which is rented and a commodity and getting cheaper by the quarter plus your context which is owned by you and unique"
- **Better models raise the value of your library**: Answer to the "next release makes this obsolete" objection: if the moat is context, a smarter model extracts more from it. Quote: "a better model makes your library worth more because a smarter reader extracts more from the same books"
- **Retrieval is not the product**: Being "just RAG" is a weak answer; value sits in what gets written down, enriched, linked, promoted and arbitrated. Quote: "retrieval is easy being worth retrieving from is the product"
- **Memory needs hygiene**: A memory product without provenance, contradiction checks and pruning compounds errors. Quote: "a brain nobody curates is a garbage dump with great search"
- **Latent vs deterministic split**: Check that taste and judgment go to the model while arithmetic, SQL and schedules go to code; confusing them causes agent failures. Quote: "there are exactly two answers and confusing them causes every agent failure I've ever seen"
- **Skill file test**: A process is automatable if a smart intern could follow the written instructions. Quote: "if a smart intern could follow it an agent can run it"
- **Revenue per person**: Track output per head as the new health metric of AI-native companies. Quote: "revenue per person did not exist before not in software not in oil not in railroads"
- **Scratch your own itch is now nearly free**: Build the tool for an audience of one, then watch whether other people ask for it. Quote: "some of your tools for one will turn out to be entire companies you'll know because other people start begging for them"
- **Compounding discipline**: Look for teams that capture learning after every task rather than throwing context away. Quote: "if you have to ask for something twice you failed"
- **Unscalable things at scale**: PG's two rules still govern; agents let one founder do the unscalable at scale. Quote: "agents are how one founder now does unscalable things at scale"
- **Ownership of expertise**: Check who owns the encoded judgment, the employee or the company; a startup is a way to make your own skill files work for you. Quote: "own your skills because if you don't your job becomes a skill file"
- **Custody as security model**: Answer to "what if it leaks": hold the keys yourself rather than scatter data across vendors. Quote: "custody is the security model"
- **Compounding curve is flat at first**: Expect weeks of toy results before the flywheel; most quit in week two. Quote: "most people who try this will quit this in week two"
- **Unscalable personal problem as proof**: Highest-conviction use is a personal, high-stakes problem nobody else will build for (a father's repo for his son's rare epilepsy). Quote: "nobody was coming to build that for him so he built it"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What context do you hold that no model has, and does it compound daily? — Answer is "our prompts" or the model itself.
- If the next model release doubles quality, does your product get better or disappear? — Disappears.
- Who owns the skills and data once an employee or customer leaves? — Unclear ownership, or the vendor locks it in.
- What is revenue per person today and what is your headcount plan? — Linear hiring to growth.
- Which parts of your workflow are deterministic code and which are model judgment? — Everything is prompts.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Product is a rented model wrapped in a chat window with no owned context — "a subscription you rent", resets and can get "a lobotomy on someone else's schedule".
- Memory without curation — confident, wrong, untraceable answers.
- Founder who cannot name a task done repeatedly and automated through a written skill.

## Green flags
- Founders treating AI as a workforce, not autocomplete.
- Small team with high revenue per person (examples in source: 15 people at $15 million annualized; about 40 people at $60 million annualized).
- Tools built for one user that others ask to borrow.

## Named examples
- YC Winter 25 batch: a quarter of companies had code bases that were 95% AI generated (speaker's claim); batch on track to be among the fastest growing.
- Emergent (summer 24 batch): public launch to nine figures of revenue in eight months, 15 people at $15M annualized.
- Retail (winter 24): $60 million annualized with about 40 people.
- GBrain / GStack: speaker's open-source personal brain and agent coding framework.
- Spinoza: used as the narrative of declining the thousand gilders offer, i.e. refusing the comfortable salary to stop building.

## Tensions
- Contradicts "wait for the next model" thinking (bitter lesson crowd): here better models strengthen context-based moats.
- The speaker's own incentive: YC's CEO says he open-sources everything because he does not have to monetise it; do not copy that go-to-market for a company that must earn revenue.
- The 400x productivity figure is self-reported and the speaker himself discounts it to 8x at the floor.
