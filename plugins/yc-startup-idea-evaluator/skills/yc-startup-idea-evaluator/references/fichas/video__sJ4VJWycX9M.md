## One-line thesis
The scarce resource in the AI era shifts from intelligence and agency to vision and ambition; win by holding a non-consensus conviction early and riding the steepest long-lived exponential.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Non-consensus conviction**: Check whether the founder holds a belief about the world that almost nobody agrees with yet and has worked on it long before it became popular. Good: "toiling in obscurity" for years. Bad: idea picked because the topic is hot in the press. Quote: "you need to develop conviction in a set of beliefs that nobody else um agrees with"
- **Early truth, not herd**: Test whether the business decisions come from the founder's own compass or from what the crowd says. Quote: "you can't base your business decisions based on what everyone else is saying around you like if you go too much with the herd you will get immensely confused"
- **First-principles origin**: The idea should come from simple true statements about the world, not from headlines. Wang's Scale origin: to train a model you needed compute, code and data, and only data had no button to press. Quote: "for two out of these three things you could just press a button online and get them and then for the last one for data there was like no effective way"
- **Unsexy but obvious**: A good idea can be dismissed by investors for years while revenue grows; investors who never used the technology misjudge it. Check for real revenue despite investor skepticism. Quote: "in the first many years of scale data was very unsexy still"
- **Right idea, wrong timing**: Founders should test whether an idea is early. Wang's first idea (AI agent for medical care) was correct long-term but the timing was wrong. Quote: "it was a great example of an idea that I think will ultimately exist like I think we're even seeing it now"
- **Steepest, longest exponential**: Ask which exponential the company rides, whether it has the steepest curve and will last longest; a boring starting point is fine. Quote: "try to identify what is the what is the exponential in the world that has both the steepest curve and will go the longest"
- **Adoption bottleneck, not model progress**: The opportunity is diffusion of existing models into the economy, so evaluate whether the startup captures adoption, not whether models improve. Quote: "the bottleneck is not the progress of the AI models the bottleneck is diffusing that through the rest of the world"
- **Goliath vs Goliath**: With agents a startup need not be a clever small David; test whether the plan uses agents ambitiously enough to beat incumbents. Quote: "if you properly embrace AI agents and um figure out the way to leverage their strengths in the most like ambitious ways you can easily out compete incumbents"
- **Agentic feedback loop with a metric**: Look for a loop where agents optimise an explicit metric or eval with far more token spend than a human team could afford. Quote: "if you can develop the right agentic loop and you have the right eval or the right metric for the agents to optimize you can have a swarm of agents"
- **Mundane mechanics**: The working machinery is plain (skills, markdown files, cron jobs, goal); be wary of magic claims. Quote: "it's always funny how uh mundane everything is once you really dig into it"
- **Each wave is bigger**: Wang's claim that each AI wave is about 10x the prior one (self-driving, chatbots, coding agents); the best AI products are not built yet. Quote: "every wave is 10 times bigger than the past wave"
- **Vision as scarce input**: Check that the founder has a concrete picture of how the world should look in 5 to 10 years and drive to push through the grind. Quote: "the scarce resource isn't going to be intelligence or agency i really think it's going to be vision and ambition"
- **Founder learning rate**: Nobody is good at starting a company at the start; judge the improvement rate. Quote: "for all entrepreneurs you start out pretty shitty at everything and um the whole game is how do you develop yourself to continuously improve"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What do you believe about this market that most smart people disagree with today? — Answer is a consensus view or a trend from the news.
- Which exponential does this ride and why will it last? — No curve named, or a fad curve.
- Is the timing right, or is this a correct idea that is too early? — Founder cannot say what changed.
- What is the metric your agents optimise, and how much would you spend in tokens to move it? — No measurable loop.
- If models stopped improving today, is there still a business? — Business depends on the next model release.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Idea chosen because the space is hot in the press — Wang: you could not have started Scale from the Wall Street Journal.
- Founder steered by investor opinion or the herd — leads to confusion and ending up "nowhere".
- Ignoring the timing question — his own first idea died on timing.

## Green flags
- Strong revenue while investors still call the market unsexy.
- Founder with a non-consensus belief stated as simple facts about the world.
- Agentic loop tied to a clear eval or metric.
- Founder who worked inside a company before founding (Wang recommends it to learn how companies work).

## Named examples
- Scale AI: data labelling bet made before LLMs; investors called it undurable then wrote think pieces about data later.
- Wang's first idea (AI agent for medical care): right idea, wrong timing; Jared Friedman told them it would not go anywhere.
- Meta Muse Spark: claimed about 8x cheaper than Opus and comparable on agentic flows with skill files (speaker's claim).

## Tensions
- "Vision and ambition" as the scarce resource sits against the lean advice of starting from a narrow customer problem; here the source stresses conviction first, though it also grounds the idea in a concrete first-principles gap.
- Speaker argues model-progress debates are a waste of time; other sources in the corpus tell founders to bet against what the next model will absorb.
