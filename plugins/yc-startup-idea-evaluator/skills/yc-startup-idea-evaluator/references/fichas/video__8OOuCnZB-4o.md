## One-line thesis
Experience becomes the edge in the agent era: veterans who know the customer, the idea maze and what to ask of "boundless intelligence" can use agents as hundreds of clones, and the lasting product is an opinionated, always-on harness for an outcome, not a general model.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Expertise steers intelligence**: Quote: "you need to have a certain amount of expertise to know what to do with this boundless intelligence that's imbued in the model"
- **Founder-market fit for a vertical agent**: Quote: "I started as a CTO of Web Flow but then I also led our marketing teams i also started our sales teams"
- **What stays hard when everyone can code**: Quote: "the thing that hasn't changed is what to focus on and how to how to actually mold it"
- **General models vs purpose-built outcome**: Quote: "there's just going to be a a big need for something that is purpose-built to help a customer achieve an outcome"
- **Harness plus skills plus code plus opinionated database**: Quote: "you're getting a database out of a box that is very opinionated around website use cases CRM use cases"
- **Beat DIY with coding agents on integrations and domain knowledge**: Quote: "claude code doesn't know how to connect to any of those things out of the box"
- **Curated data corpus as a moat**: Quote: "we've created 3,500 prompts for web designs that then ploy takes inspiration from"
- **Anti-slop through context**: Quote: "you can't necessarily eliminate all of it with all the prompts and guard rails and steering"
- **Deterministic consistency on top of generation**: Quote: "a purely deterministic method to take a existing website and to not just create a design system but to create all the components"
- **Customer choice: avoid engineers as customers**: they switch tools on a whim. Quote: "software engineers are one of like the worst customers to to sell to and they they can change tools on a whim"
- **Pick a customer with a true pain point**: Quote: "just go and pick a customer that has like a true true pain point and just really really focus on that"
- **Start from the first step users take**: homepage as source of truth, like Rippling's offer letter generator. Quote: "the very first first thing with a website is the homepage"
- **Agents as customers**: Quote: "if the agents choose you that's actually big and you're going to win huge"
- **CLI plus skills over MCP when scope is broad**: Quote: "we're going to do a CLI with skills"
- **Broaden the persona with AI**: Quote: "at Webflow we focused on one persona that persona was this like freelance web designer you know just like there's probably only 50,000 of them"
- **Scale of persona now**: Quote: "with Ploy you know we're essentially solving for tens of millions of people"
- **Clone-yourself leverage**: Quote: "it's basically like 400 to a,000 clones of myself"
- **Experienced vs young founders**: Quote: "for experienced founders adopt a little bit more of that bravvada m and that risk appetite"
- **Marketing loop competitive pressure**: Quote: "if you're not using things like ploy then your competitors will win"
- **Dream-cycle autonomy**: nightly agent suggestions from traffic and search data. Quote: "every single night we look at all the traffic we check your Google search console"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What domain expertise is encoded in your product that a coding agent cannot reproduce with prompts? — Red flag: only a UI over a general model.
- Can your customer get the same outcome by pasting your idea into a coding agent? — Red flag: yes in an afternoon.
- Who is your buyer and do they churn on a whim? — Red flag: engineers or tool-switchers as primary customers.
- What do agents do for the customer while they sleep? — Red flag: dashboards that wait for a human.
- Would an agent choose your product and can it sign up on its own? — Red flag: human-only onboarding.
- What does the first-wedge product look like (offer-letter generator, homepage)? — Red flag: only a huge unfocused vision.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Pure vibe-code wrapper with no integrations, data or memory (the host's description "kind of looks like a lot of the other things you might use to vibe code").
- Customers in a "lowest common denominator" market where tokens are the only competition.
- Content for content's sake (SEO spam): "you can't create a hundred websites and then expect Google to think that hey you're authoritative source".
- Experienced founders paralyzed by scar tissue.

## Green flags
- About 12% of the YC batch using the product (his figure).
- Founder with a decade or more of relevant operating experience.
- Opinionated data layer, curated lookbook, deterministic component extraction.
- Product that makes agents' discovery of the customer possible (AEO, schema markup).

## Named examples
- Ploy (AI website and marketing platform), Webflow, Posterous, Automatic, Script/YouTube for documents (old sites recreated as demos).
- Parker Conrad's Rippling/Zenefits: wedge via offer-letter generator; two years in a basement then launch of a full package.
- Cursor "Composer": example customer brand used in demo.
- OpenClaw "dream cycle" and cron-style agents.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the "only young founders can use the tools" narrative of the ChatGPT-era batches; says experience is now an edge.
- Contradicts lore on MCP: prefers CLI plus skills.
- Host claims about "400 to 1,000 clones" and "4-5 million lines of code" are promotional and not verified.
- Webflow did one narrow persona; Ploy "boils the ocean": contradicts the usual advice to start narrow, justified by AI cost collapse.
