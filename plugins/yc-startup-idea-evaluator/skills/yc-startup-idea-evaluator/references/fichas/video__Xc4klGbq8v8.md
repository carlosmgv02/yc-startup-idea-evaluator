## One-line thesis
In deep tech, speed of iteration decides success and speed is built by boring infrastructure (purchasing, budgeting, hiring, reviews); founders must not delegate judgment, because average judgment cannot reach outlier outcomes.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Iteration rate decides**: choose the approach with the shorter learning loop, all else equal. Quote: "if you can learn one thing every week and there's a competitor that's learning a thing every month they will they will never matter"
- **Speed comes from infrastructure**: Quote: "speed determines success and failure and speed is determined by infrastructure"
- **Deep-tech failure mode is organization, not technology**: Quote: "it's uncommon that these deep tech companies that fail because the technology doesn't work they fail because once you end up with this organization of hundreds of people"
- **Budget before purchase approval**: spending review belongs in budgeting, not in approving each purchase. Quote: "spending review has to come earlier you have to have some concept of budgeting"
- **Cost attribution per experiment**: if nobody knows what an experiment costs, it is treated as free. Quote: "if nobody knows how much an experiment costs like every time you like grow up a new cell line or every time we make a new a new probe in in the fab how much does that loop cost nobody knows therefore experiments are free"
- **Do not delegate judgment**: Quote: "you cannot delegate your judgment as as the CEO you must always make decisions that make sense to you no matter how much momentum or inertia alternatives seem to have"
- **Average is not enough**: Quote: "the successful companies are the exceptions by becoming an average that is not good enough"
- **Action produces information**: when stuck, act. Quote: "action produces information"
- **Raise to run the experiment**: size the round to the next value inflection point, then double it. Quote: "figure out what it costs to actually run the experiment raise twice that"
- **Some ideas deserve $50M or zero, not $5M**: Quote: "there are definitely some ideas that are worth funding with $50 million or zero dollars but not $5 million"
- **Push for revenue early**: Quote: "people should push for profitability sooner than they often think that they need to"
- **Hiring from the scene that produced the startup**: Quote: "the best hiring comes from within your network people that you've worked with before you know are good"
- **Distributed hiring judgment plus a conversion check**: Quote: "at least 25% conversion to an offer because otherwise it is just you're going to waste too much of your time doing on-sites"
- **Hire for judgment, horsepower, agency**: Quote: "really looking for three things judgment horsepower and agency"
- **AI-resistant, scorable homework**: Quote: "things that um don't saturate have a very high ceiling and are naturally scorable to two or three numbers"
- **Continuous peer feedback instead of annual reviews**: Quote: "knowing how this person turned out would you vote again today for their hire"
- **Evidence of exceptional ability is a legible competitive game**: Quote: "the best evidence of exceptional ability is are winning at legible competitive games"
- **Internal tools now feasible via agents**: Quote: "historically software has been so expensive you would have had to buy it"
- **Make company context available to agents**: Quote: "gather all of the context all the stuff happening in your company and be able to make that available efficiently to agents"
- **AI multiplies regulated-work throughput**: Quote: "historically AI has totally transformed it i mean we can very quickly look up all the standards"
- **Small cross-disciplinary teams hold the whole problem**: Quote: "it's better to have a smaller team that can fit more of the problem in their heads and then compress it together"
- **Industry vs academia**: Quote: "when a field really starts to work industry can just marshall such larger levels of resources and can just move so much faster"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- How many learning cycles per month does your approach allow versus the alternative? — Red flag: cycles measured in quarters.
- Do you know what one experiment/iteration costs in dollars? — Red flag: no cost attribution, "experiments are free".
- What is the next value inflection point and how much does it cost to reach? — Red flag: raising an amount chosen to look "reasonable" to VCs.
- How do employees buy things and hire, and who bottlenecks the process? — Red flag: founder approves everything or a process takes two weeks per order.
- Which decisions did you make against advice? — Red flag: all decisions are consensus; judgment untested.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Pedigree founders with no execution systems; "die on the vine".
- Underfunding an experiment so the result is ambiguous.
- Spending review applied per purchase (slows highly paid staff; their time dwarfs savings).
- Annual 360 reviews that surface only what you already knew.
- Treating startup advice as a checklist: "there are no general principles".

## Green flags
- Short iteration cycle; software everywhere (purchasing, manufacturing steps, hiring in one database).
- Revenue focus even in deep tech, which widens the investor base.
- Founders who can hold the whole problem across disciplines.

## Named examples
- Science: retinal prosthesis (clinical trials in six countries, approved device in Europe), internal software "Helix".
- Eigen reviews: PageRank-like weighted peer votes; dropout detects voting cliques.
- SpaceX "Warp Speed", YC internal software, Facebook internal tools.
- Neuralink: lessons from working next to someone with "empirically excellent judgment".

## Tensions
- Contradicts "never build what you can buy": says custom internal software is now reasonable even at seed ("if you raise a series A and tell investors you'll vibe code a purchasing system, any reasonable board will ask what you're thinking"; he controls the company).
- Contradicts generic "startup advice": says rely on your own judgment, not advice.
- Capital-intensive biotech: he would not recommend it if you have a choice; a decade of life.
