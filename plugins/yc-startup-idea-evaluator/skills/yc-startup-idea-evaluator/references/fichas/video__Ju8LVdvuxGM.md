## One-line thesis
In the AI wave, global companies can be built from anywhere because the winner is whoever understands the technology 10x better and ships fastest; ideas are found by tinkering and building projects, not on whiteboards.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Edge of the technology over GTM polish**: In this wave check whether the founder lives at the technical edge, not whether the business model is polished. Quote: "this wave is more about are you living at the edge of the technology and not as much about do you understand the right go to market uh right business model etc"
- **AI is not local**: Contrast with the mobile wave (hyperlocal network effects, tokenised labour). Check whether the idea can be sold globally rather than city by city. Quote: "the AI revolution is not local uh that revolution led to hyperlocal network effects"
- **Cold outreach works if the product is better**: No warm intro or US presence needed; buyers want outcomes. Quote: "this is not about where the budget goes this is about whose solution is better and can drive better outcomes"
- **Build limited by learning, not building**: With coding agents the founder bottleneck is learning pace. Quote: "you are no longer limited by your ability to build you're limited by the pace with which you can learn"
- **Work on what is barely good enough for the models**: Follow curiosity to the frontier; bottlenecks you find become ideas. Quote: "work on the things that are barely good enough for the models to do and in so doing you're going to beat benchmarks you're going to establish new heights"
- **Good ideas are not obvious up front**: You need to be building to notice the real idea; the first idea is rarely the one. Quote: "a good startup idea is not obvious right it's like you have to kind of be in the field building something to notice the really good idea"
- **Second mover with a better product**: Find something that works and do it better unless it has strong network effects. Quote: "find something that's kind of working and then do it better than them and then beat them and unless that first thing had incredible network effects which few things do"
- **Small team beating bigger rivals**: Example of closing DoorDash against competitors with several hundred employees while having eight. Quote: "he was competing against several companies that had several hundred employees and he had eight"
- **Let the tokens rip**: Test whether the founder explores what changes when inference cost is ignored (10,000 unit tests, $5 per email reply); unmet needs surface. Quote: "you can get a much better output if you basically just don't worry about the uh the cost of inference"
- **Build for models 6 to 12 months ahead**: Road map should anticipate next model capabilities. Quote: "if you try and build for the models 6 months ahead a year ahead um or just think through what the models can be capable of"
- **Cost-of-compute will fall**: Assume price per unit of intelligence keeps dropping. Quote: "I think it's very safe to assume cost of compute per um level of intelligence is going to go down a lot"
- **Clarity in the application**: Founders should be understandable at a glance. Quote: "clarity is most important if we can't understand what people are actually building um that's not going to work right"
- **Taste is intention**: Taste is design choices backed by customer insight and pace to those insights, not looks. Quote: "it's fundamentally more about intention are you building products and and showing demos that are intentional"
- **Relentless resourcefulness and customer obsession**: Founders who lead the company through crazy times; customer talk was the consistent theme in every founder story. Quote: "being relentlessly resourceful and having taste as expressed through customer obsession it's kind of this this consistent theme through all of them"
- **Project definition**: A project is two people building something not assigned to them and getting someone to use it. Quote: "a project is when two people build something that was not assigned to them and get someone to use it"
- **Surround yourself with AI natives**: Advice from non-AI-native people is dangerous in this era. Quote: "in AI actually it's very dangerous to follow the advice of people who are not AI native because they're just not in the game with you"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Have you built a project, with another person, that someone actually uses? — Only classes, internships or whiteboard exercises.
- What did you find was missing while building that you could not see on a whiteboard? — Idea came from brainstorming.
- Who is already doing this and what will you do measurably better? — Assumes no competition, or relies on network effects that do not exist.
- Why would a US buyer take a cold email from you? — Plan depends on a warm intro.
- What would your product do if tokens were free? — Never tested the high-spend version.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Idea is a whiteboard exercise never built or used — the source says you cannot see the good idea from there.
- Waiting for warm connections or SF residency before selling — the source calls this a preconception that no longer holds.
- Following non-AI-native conventional advice — "cookie cutter advice".

## Green flags
- Founder who cold-emails buyers in a hard vertical (an Indian student selling to US insurers by email).
- Young founders tinkering at the edge of what the models can do.
- Teams of 2 with a used project.
- Founders without US connections who win on product (Giga, Emergent).

## Named examples
- SuperDaily: Punit's grocery delivery startup; he said "two engineers but like I was one of them", about $100 million annual revenue, exited to Swiggy.
- Giga: founder closed DoorDash with eight people against several-hundred-employee rivals.
- Emergent: scaling fast as a better second mover in AI coding.
- Open Code (YC): built on open-source models, recommended as cheap access.
- Meesho (spelled "Misho" [sic]): voice AI to bring the next billion users online, requiring low-cost open models.
- Gary Tan (speaker refers to him): reportedly spends thousands of dollars per day on tokens.

## Tensions
- "Ideas are not what we invest in" and "the first idea is rarely the one" sit against evaluating ideas up front; the source says you evaluate the founder and rate of learning.
- "Let it rip on tokens" conflicts with lean budgeting, though the speaker admits it is a privilege and open-source models are an alternative.
- Second-mover advice contrasts with the usual "be first"; it holds only when the incumbent lacks strong network effects.
