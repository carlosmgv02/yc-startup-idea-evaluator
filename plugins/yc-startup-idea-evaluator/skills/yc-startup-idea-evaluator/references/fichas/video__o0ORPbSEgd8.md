## One-line thesis
Legora (legal AI, YC) won by learning an unfamiliar industry fast, betting that models keep improving, freezing sales until the product was reliable, and building a high-intensity culture; domain expertise was not required, willingness to learn was.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Domain expertise is optional, learning speed is not**: non-lawyer founders built the category leader. Quote: "i think that you need a willingness to learn about the market which we did very very early by you know exposing ourselves and spending lots of time with lawyers"
- **Caveat on domain expertise**: it may matter for deep-science markets. Quote: "maybe if you're building in quantum computing or in um fusion that's a good idea but uh legal was easy enough for us to pick up"
- **Pay-for-lunch customer discovery**: cold email and LinkedIn asking for lunch, offering to pay the lawyer's hourly fee. Quote: "we offered to pay their hourly fee to have lunch with us and many of them took us up on that offer"
- **Old software as opportunity**: incumbent software built in the 90s signals room for an LLM product. Quote: "the software that we were up against looked like it had built in the '9s"
- **Bet on model improvement, not fine-tuning**: in 2022 to 2023 the tribal knowledge was to fine-tune; they bet the models would improve and focused on delivering value to the market. Quote: "by just betting that the models will keep improving and what you should be focused on is how can you deliver that the value that the models generate to your"
- **Build for today plus one step**: Quote: "you don't have to build for the world over here like you need to build for the world today and maybe like one step ahead"
- **Early wedge may be weak but real**: first pitch was a compliance gap versus ChatGPT. Quote: "we are like chat GPT but we are compliant in Europe like that was the pitch"
- **Incumbent copilot risk can fail**: they feared Microsoft Copilot for lawyers in Word and Outlook; it did not work well. Quote: "turns out like it didn't work and the um you know ability for us then to still build a lot of value was huge"
- **Freeze sales to fix trust**: with an unforgiving buyer (lawyers) one failed demo is fatal, so they paused sales for six months. Quote: "when you work with lawyers you only really get one chance to get it right"
- **Interest income exceeding customer revenue is a bad sign**: Quote: "there was actually a month following that that we made more money from interest rates on those 35 million than we did from customers"
- **Product manifesto over democratic voting**: too many chefs, then one document. Quote: "we would make democratic votes in the entire team and for anyone who has built software before you know that if you have too many chefs in the kitchen that"
- **Hiring for slope, not logos**: Quote: "looking for fancy logos on resumeumés or what we like to call y inter like y intercept"
- **Winner-takes-most mentality**: Quote: "there is only winning everything else is losing in a winner takes all market or winner takes most market"
- **Evals as core IP**: use-case evals let you route between models by cost and intelligence. Quote: "one of the core IPs and muscles that I encourage as many of you as possible to build is uh the ability to evalu use cases because that is the"
- **Intelligence vs cost depends on the vertical**: in law, token spend is tiny relative to human expertise, so buyers want maximum intelligence. Quote: "in law you actually want the you know most amount of intelligence quite often because the fraction of token spend or software spend compared to like you know human expertise"
- **Proactive agents**: moving from prompt-response to trigger-driven agents. Quote: "how does one lawyer produce the outcomes of a team of 10 lawyers with the help of Lora"
- **Speed on customer feedback as superpower**: Quote: "your ability as a smaller company to be fast versus your incumbent competition is the superpower"
- **Space vs problem**: they picked a space, not a specific problem; contrasts with solving a specific ROI problem and expanding. Quote: "we picked a space more than we picked a problem which I think is like two different ways of going about it"
- **Peer group sets ambition**: Quote: "the best way to become more ambitious is to have a peer group of uh other very ambitious people"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What did you do to learn the buyer's world before building? — No customer conversations, or relies on borrowed expertise.
- What breaks if the model improves 10x, and what breaks if it does not? — Moat depends on a fine-tune or a model gap.
- Does one failed demo kill your account? If so, when did you last pause growth to fix quality? — Pushes sales while product is unreliable.
- What are your evals, by use case? — None.
- What is your equivalent of "chatgpt but compliant": what narrow wedge got you the first sale? — Generic positioning only.
- Is the buyer's cost dominated by human expertise or by software spend? — Optimizing for cheapest tokens in a high-stakes market.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Sales push before the product is trustworthy to a risk-averse buyer — "you are toast".
- Feature decisions by vote.
- Building a model fine-tune instead of value delivery on improving base models.
- Hiring primarily for logo pedigree.

## Green flags
- Founders customer-facing, paid-for-lunch discovery with hundreds of lawyers in the early days.
- Embedded in a design-partner law firm (moved into its offices).
- Evals built early and benchmark kept internal until it could be useful.
- Customer love channel with concrete quotes.

## Named examples
- Legora: 1 to 100 million ARR in about 18 months; from three engineers to over 750 people; Benchmark invested $9.51 million (as stated); 6-month sales freeze; Jude Law ad campaign through persistence.
- Mannheimer Swartling: Nordic law firm used as the first design partner.
- Bloomberg's own law model: the fine-tuning path they declined.
- Microsoft Copilot: feared incumbent that did not deliver for lawyers.
- Finn (customer support, about $1 per resolved ticket): contrast on cost versus intelligence.

## Tensions
- Contradicts advice that vertical software requires domain expertise ("a Swedish VC" passed because there were no lawyers).
- Uses the large-seed pile as evidence of the freeze: a counterexample to "always be selling".
- Admits the idea was a space, not a sharp problem, versus the YC norm of a specific problem with ROI.
