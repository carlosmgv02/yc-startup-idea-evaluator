## One-line thesis
Price B2B software from the value you deliver (about a third of it), using cost only as a floor and competition as a reason to differentiate, then iterate; do not over-optimize early.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Value equation first**: can the founders write, with a champion, step-by-step value (cost saving, time saving or revenue increase) that the customer has challenged? Quote: "you write down with this Champion uh what they expect your product to do for them what value it's going to deliver uh to their company"
- **Three value types**: Quote: "you're normally saving time which is cost or reducing cost directly or increasing Revenue those are typically the three things that companies care about"
- **Price at a share of value**: Quote: "I typically pick somewhere between 25 and 50% of the value you're delivering so they keep roughly 2/3 you keep roughly a third"
- **Worked example**: 100 agents at $100,000 fully loaded = $10 million; a 20% reduction = $2 million saving; charge about 700k. Quote: "that's $2 million of potential cost saving"
- **Value equation doubles as pilot success metric**: Quote: "the value equation tells you the the success metrics that you need to prove during a pilot process"
- **Cost is only a floor**: never cost-plus; Quote: "you never start with cost some people like to do a cost plus a margin pricing and it just always ends up with you underpricing your software cost should only"
- **Margin target**: Quote: "you should be aiming for software margins of like 80 or 90%"
- **Credits are cash cost**: Quote: "you should treat those as a cash cost don't assume you'll have unlimited credits forever it'll totally mess up your margins"
- **Value below cost means bad business**: Quote: "if however you've come up with a value equation and and your share of it which only comes in at 150k and your cost a 200k you're in a bad"
- **Pricing at or below cost is a land-grab bet**: Quote: "you're really betting that the costs are going to come down dramatically in future"
- **No price wars**: Quote: "competing solely on pricing really is not a winning maneuver you don't want to get into a head-to-head bidding war for a commodity product"
- **Differentiate**: Quote: "it can't be an apples for apples comparison your product needs to be differentiated"
- **Commodity margin**: Quote: "if there's extreme competition in an industry for a commodity product that's a product where they're all basically the same all the margin gets driven out"
- **Mirror how they pay today**: Quote: "if you can mirror the way they are used to paying for other software the better you'll do"
- **Keep pricing simple**: Quote: "over complicating pricing will kill a sales process"
- **Committed recurring over usage-based**: Quote: "in general committed recurring Revenue that's monthly recurring revenue or annual recurring revenue is preferable to usage based pricing"
- **Usage to commitment conversion**: Quote: "start with usage based pricing for new customers um run the the the contract for a month or two and see what usage is and then offer to move them"
- **Signing authority**: ask what the champion can approve alone. Quote: "ask your Champion what amount they're able to personally sign off without having to get additional approvals from the CFO or the legal team"
- **Enterprise tier gates**: Quote: "often it's things like uh sock 2 audit reports or single sign on or audit logs or compliance reports or data being kept in certain geographies"
- **Price vs sales channel (5:1)**: Quote: "a good rule of thumb is about a 5:1 ratio between new signed ARR and total compensation for a salesperson including commission"
- **Short pilots**: Quote: "offering a really long free trial or a pilot is counterproductive uh the customer not actually bought into using the product"
- **Annual contract with money-back**: Quote: "push your customers to sign up for an annual contract from the very start but with a 30-day or 60-day money back guarantee"
- **Raise price per pitch fallback**: Quote: "increase that number by 50% for every time you pitch a new customer"
- **Right ballpark test**: Quote: "when you start to lose more than 25% of potential deals base solely on price you're now probably in the right ballp" [sic]

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What is the customer's quantified value, and did your champion challenge it? — red flag: no written value, price guessed from consumer SaaS ($19 or $49 a month)
- What does it cost you to serve one customer, including model/cloud fees without credits? — red flag: price is near or below cost; margin well under 80-90%
- What will you do when a competitor charges half? — red flag: plan is to undercut
- How do customers in this industry pay for similar software today? — red flag: no knowledge; exotic pricing structure
- How much can your champion sign without the CFO? — red flag: pilot priced above signing authority with no plan
- Does the contract size fund a sales team? — red flag: tiny contracts with an outbound sales motion
- What is the pilot success metric and length? — red flag: long free pilot with no criteria
- How many deals have you lost on price? — red flag: zero lost deals (underpricing) or well over 25%

## Red flags / kill signals
- Cost-plus pricing — "always ends up with you underpricing"
- Value share that does not cover cost — "you're in a bad business"
- Treating cloud/AI credits as free — margins will break when they run out
- Head-to-head price war on a commodity product — airline industry example, 2.7% net margin
- Pure usage-based revenue — "your Revenue just falls off a cliff in a in a bad month and investors are really worryed [sic] about that"
- Deals so small a salesperson would need 42 a month — "not a true account executive or outbound sales team"
- Every deal closing immediately — "you're almost certainly underpricing"
- Faking a bigger company on the website

## Green flags
- Written value equation owned by the champion, usable as CFO justification
- Gross margin 80-90%
- Differentiated product (niche, integrations, specific industries)
- Short pilot with clear metrics, or annual contract with money-back guarantee
- Enterprise tier gating compliance, SSO, audit logs, data residency, priced up to 10x higher
- Founders playing to startup strengths such as founder phone numbers and 24/7 support

## Named examples
- Customer service AI tool with 100 agents: $2 million saving, about $700k price
- Airline industry: commodity, "2.7% net profit margin"
- OpenAI and Anthropic: keep dramatically cutting costs, possible argument for pricing lower now (but risky)
- Salesforce and Oracle: cannot offer founder-level 24/7 availability

## Tensions
- Says to price at a third of value, but admits fallback is to pick a number and raise 50% per pitch: "over optimizing for pricing early is a big mistake".
- Against developer instinct to publish prices: for Enterprise, contact sales because value differs per customer; publishing "you are certainly leaving money on the table".
- Allows pricing at or below cost in land-grab races but calls it "very very dangerous".
- Prefers committed recurring revenue to usage-based, against the AI-era instinct for pure usage pricing.
