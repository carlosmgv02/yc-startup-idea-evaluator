## One-line thesis
A tarpit idea is a small, specific set of ideas that constantly attract founders and almost always fail; the set is not static (new technology such as LLMs can untar a pit) and the founder must explain what changed.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Tarpit definition**: many founders apply with the same idea repeatedly and the idea space never works. Quote: "we would see a lot of Founders applying with the same idea over and over and over again and often we would fund many of them and often the idea"
- **Tarpit is not just "bad idea"**: it is a small subset. Quote: "tarpet ideas are a smallish set of ideas yes there's lots of ideas that are neither good nor bad they're just normal"
- **Everyone has it test**: Quote: "if some if other Founders don't come up with them all the time constantly it is not a tarpit idea it has to be it has to be something that"
- **Praise is a signal**: friends loving the idea is suspicious. Quote: "most startup ideas when you tell your friends and they being honest they're like that's not a good idea tarpet ideas get like a lot of Praise"
- **Tried a lot and nothing changed**: Quote: "it's been done a lot a lot of people have failed and nothing has changed"
- **Research history**: founders avoid research to not learn what they do not want to hear. Quote: "a lot of Founders you know avoid research to not learn things they don't want to learn"
- **Technology change reopens ideas**: Quote: "what I love to see is when a Founder can explain to me why the new technology might make something accomplishable that wasn't accomplishable before"
- **Acknowledge past failures**: Quote: "what I hate to see is a Founder that's not even acknowledging that like many people have tried and failed before"
- **Connect tech to problem**: Quote: "if you can't draw any connection between the two technology and why your problem can be solved today and it's one of these ideas that have been tried many times"
- **Copilot for X is common, not a tarpit**: Quote: "these are not tarpits because not enough people have tried them exactly but they are common ideas"
- **Current tech addresses only current problems**: Quote: "whatever current set of technology is available can address only a current set of problems and like some problems are going to fall outside of that set and every time"
- **Flavor 1, make the world work differently**: Quote: "a belief that the world should work differently than it does better and this is all coming from a good place but it's basically wow the way X works is"
- **Flavor 1 diagnosis**: Quote: "it's just hard to change human Behavior"
- **Flavor 2, arbitrage or get rich quick**: Quote: "like I have some secret knowledge and like I can get very rich very fast"
- **Too good to be true**: Quote: "if you're getting too much positive feedback if it feels like why does this already exist it seems so easy that's not like the shape of things that we see"
- **Talk to users first**: Quote: "you should talk to users and get a customer"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Have you talked to users? — red flag: asks the partner whether the idea is a tarpit instead of testing it
- Who has tried this before and why did they fail? — red flag: unaware, or "history starts when I was born"
- What is different now that makes it possible, and why LLMs/new tech? — red flag: no link between technology and the problem
- Does this require everyone to adopt a new behavior at once? — red flag: yes (network of friends coordinating)
- Is the idea an arbitrage window or "secret knowledge"? — red flag: rich-quick timing play
- Why does nobody already do this if it is so easy? — red flag: no answer
- Is it "[X] copilot" generated from first principles? — red flag: no customer-specific insight beyond the template

## Red flags / kill signals
- Idea attracts constant applications and constant praise — tarpit pattern
- Founder ignores prior attempts — no explanation of what changed
- Requires coordinated human-behavior change (everyone installs an app to hang out) — "it's the perfect example of something where like nobody would say no" but nobody adopts
- Arbitrage or get-rich-fast motivation tied to a moment (adtech, Wall Street Bets-inspired investing advisory) — mercenary flavor
- Did not talk to users before asking whether the idea is a tarpit

## Green flags
- Founder explains what technology change makes the previously failing idea feasible
- Founder acknowledges previous failures and has a theory
- Open to "classic" tarpit ideas when new tech credibly changes the picture
- Talked to users and has a customer
