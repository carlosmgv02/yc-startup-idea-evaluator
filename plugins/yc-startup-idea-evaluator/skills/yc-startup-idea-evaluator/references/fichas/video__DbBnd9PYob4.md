## One-line thesis
Building at the product layer next to frontier labs is viable if you are model-agnostic, horizontal, hold a defensibility beyond intelligence (network effects), price by usage, and raise modestly; verticalised AI wrappers lose the scaffolding advantage as models improve.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Model-agnostic as something labs cannot copy**: A lab cannot sell neutral access to rivals' models. Test whether neutrality is a customer need. Quote: "model agnostic which I think is a is a very interesting aspect of that and that's something that the labs would not be able to do"
- **Do not buy product and tokens from one vendor**: The analogy is a plant that can only connect to one energy provider. Quote: "if you were uh building a plant with machines in there and you would buy the machines from the energy provider and the plug would only work with one energy provider"
- **Best model rotates**: Usage concentrates on one provider at a time, but which one changes. Quote: "at for given period of time that provider is the best for doing work at another given point of time this other provider is the best"
- **Vertical scaffolding value disappears**: Vertical AI helped go-to-market when models were weak and needed scaffolding; that value shrinks as models improve. Quote: "that second value just disappears as the models are getting better"
- **Vertical needs a network effect**: If intelligence is commoditised, the defensible part is network effects in the vertical, not delivered intelligence. Quote: "we're going back to the advices that we used to receive in 2010 almost which is network effects"
- **Horizontal platform GTM is finicky**: Vertical gives obvious targets; horizontal platforms hit "what am I going to use it for". Quote: "when you have an horizontal platform it's super well documented uh we're not the first to do that notion air tableable"
- **Lab co-presence educates the market**: Labs educating the market helps, but you are "a tiny tiny spec". Quote: "they educate the markets uh very efficiently"
- **Convergence to coverage over depth**: Products converge on integrations and breadth rather than deep verticals; differentiate by sensibility (collaboration, multiplayer AI). Quote: "much less depth in terms of product and much more coverage in terms of where it integrates"
- **Credit-based pricing**: Flat seat pricing breaks when agent loops use more tokens; margins compress. Quote: "if you have a a flat price that just doesn't fly"
- **Margin of the labs**: Same-latency comparison with open-weight models shows about a 9x price difference, suggesting 70 to 80% lab margins (speaker's estimate), which open source may pressure. Quote: "the difference is like almost 9x"
- **Precedents of products on infrastructure that captures margin**: Companies sold a product on top of infrastructure and resold at zero price (Box, Dropbox-like); value is in a crafted, differentiated product. Quote: "there are precedents where uh companies manage to sell a product on top of an infrastructure that captures the margins"
- **Raise modestly, no coffin corner**: Raising too much without realising its potential leads to a down round with no escape. Quote: "there is a true coffin corner for companies if you raise too much and do not kind of realize the potential of the money you raised"
- **No GPU before PMF**: Product-layer companies cannot use training money. Quote: "No GPU before PMF"
- **Right product-layer raise**: They raised 5 million seed while others raised 100 or 200 million for training. Quote: "we raised five five millions which seems like silly but at the same time we knew that we building on the at the product layer we would not have been able to use that money"
- **Vision as survival fuel**: Company building is daily grind; find the thing you want to fix. Quote: "if you don't have that thing that you can really wake up in the morning and say but this is the reason reason why I'm doing it uh it's it's not sustainable"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- If a lab ships your vertical, what is your defensibility other than scaffolding? — Only prompts and workflow glue.
- Are you neutral across models, and what does the customer gain from that? — Single-vendor lock-in.
- How does your pricing behave when agent loops double token use? — Flat seat price.
- What is your gross margin after token cost, and how does it move with usage? — Margins compressing with no plan.
- Do you have a network effect in your vertical? — No, just intelligence delivered separately to each customer.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Vertical AI product with no network effect — labs entering the vertical (Anthropic going after Legora in law) is the example.
- Flat-price plans on usage that is exploding — "margins have compressed".
- Raising training-scale money without the use for it — coffin corner.

## Green flags
- Early thesis that work will be disrupted, even if the market took three years to show it.
- Developer workflow changed completely "since November 2025" (speaker's account), and other functions starting to follow.
- Modest valuations with the aim of a healthy next round.

## Named examples
- Dust: horizontal, model-agnostic, multiplayer AI platform; moving from seat-based to credit-based pricing.
- Mistral: praised for a researcher (Arthur Mensch) who grew into a CEO.
- Legora (legal AI) targeted by Anthropic, used as the vertical risk example.
- Box, Dropbox-like cases: products resold on infrastructure at no extra price; Notion and Airtable as earlier horizontal platforms.

## Tensions
- Against "verticalize AI" advice common in the prior two years; the speaker bet horizontal and says vertical value is shrinking.
- Admits the French base made series B harder; says location does not matter once things work, but it is an added friction.
- The speaker admits he was wrong about an earlier plateau; "that plateau is not happening at all".
