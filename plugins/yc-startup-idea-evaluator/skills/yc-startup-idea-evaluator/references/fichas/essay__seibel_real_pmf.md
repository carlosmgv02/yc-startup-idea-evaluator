## One-line thesis
Product/market fit means being overwhelmed by usage from happy, loyal, ideally paying customers, not having employees or funding; until then stay lean and focus on the market (a dire problem) before your solution.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Fit = overwhelmed by usage**: Quote: "you have reached product/market fit when you are overwhelmed with usage—usually to the point where you can’t even make major changes to your product because you are swamped just keeping it up and running."
- **Andreessen definition**: Quote: "The customers are buying the product just as fast as you can make it — or usage is growing just as fast as you can add more servers."
- **False signals**: headcount and funding rounds are not fit. Quote: "Founders often try to signal they’ve reached product/market by pointing to their number of employees or a major round of funding."
- **What to look for**: Quote: "What I look for is a frantic founding team trying to deal with ever-growing numbers of happy, loyal, and ideally paying customers."
- **Premature scaling check**: if fit is not proven, do not hire or raise burn. Quote: "they start hiring people, increasing burn, and optimizing their product before they’ve actually discovered what needs to be built."
- **Stay lean until fit**: Quote: "stay lean, keep burn low, and resemble a Navy SEAL team instead of an Army battalion."
- **Market before solution**: Quote: "Founders often hold too tightly onto solutions and too loosely onto problems."
- **v1 is usually wrong**: Quote: "Your unique and special v1 idea on how to solve that problem is usually wrong"
- **Founder genius = problem choice**: Quote: "Founder genius is most often expressed in choosing the right problem to solve."
- **Hair on fire**: users must be willing to try imperfect solutions (a brick instead of a hose). Quote: "You need to find problems so dire that users are willing try half-baked, v1, imperfect solutions."
- **MVP test in a good market**: Quote: "In a good market, an MVP is all you need to get your initial customers in the door, interacting with you, and offering the feedback that will inform your v2, v3, and v4."
- **Recipe**: Quote: "choose a market where users have a real, meaningful problem, launch quickly, and listen to your users."
- **After fit**: optimization and specialist hiring come only after fit. Quote: "Once you’ve actually reached product/market fit—congratulations—you can begin optimizing your core product, hiring specialists to increase your efficiency, and make strategic investments."

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Are customers buying as fast as you can serve them? Is usage overwhelming the team? — no, usage is steady or modest while headcount grows.
- Whose hair is on fire? Would they use a half-baked v1? — users who only "like" the idea, or need a polished product.
- How has your v1 changed since talking to customers? — unchanged, defended as special.
- Why are you hiring? — hiring justified by funding or plans rather than by customer pull.
- Is money from customers piling up? — no revenue or no paying customers.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Pointing to employee count or a funding round as evidence of fit.
- Hiring, raising burn and optimizing the product before discovering what needs to be built.
- Clinging to the v1 solution while loosely holding the problem.
- Problem is not dire: users will not try imperfect versions.

## Green flags
- Frantic team swamped by growing usage, can barely change the product.
- Customers happy, loyal and paying.
- Money from customers piling up.
- Small, fast team with unusually good customer service.

## Named examples
- Marc Andreessen: the definition of product/market fit and "the market pulls product out of the startup".
- Sequoia: "hair on fire" customers.
- Justin.tv / Socialcam (Seibel's own companies): author background.

## Tensions
- YC advice to launch an MVP quickly versus the claim that many founders think they have fit when they do not.
- Aligns with "hire slowly" but frames fit as an outcome, not a milestone a team can declare.
