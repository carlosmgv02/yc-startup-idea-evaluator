## One-line thesis
YC reads an application as a story about founders, product, evidence of demand and seriousness; clarity, honesty and technical talent drive selection, and interviews test whether the partners can have a productive working relationship with credible, self-aware founders who know their business.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Story test**: A good application lets the reader tell a story with characters, a beginning and an end. Quote: "it has characters it has a beginning it has an end I understand what's going on"
- **Clarity or zero**: If the basics cannot be parsed, nothing else lands. Quote: "if I can't even understand basics of what you're trying to communicate to me you're not even the ball in the ballpark of convincing me"
- **Concrete description**: Good: concrete about what it is, what it does, who uses it. Quote: "they're very concrete about what it is what it does who uses it"
- **Current state**: Reader wants what exists today. Quote: "what exactly do they have today right today at this moment in time is it built are there users"
- **Evidence of demand**: Quote: "is there any evidence that people want this they have customers they have users um are they using their own product"
- **Seriousness**: Left the job; repeat applicants who show progress. Quote: "if someone has left their job to work on this full-time that's an Evidence of seriousness"
- **Progress across attempts**: Quote: "if someone has applied multiple times and every time they apply they make more progress that's evidence of seriousness"
- **Stand-out factor**: Quote: "sometimes the founders are unique sometimes the traction is unique sometimes the ideas and idea I've never heard before"
- **Founder backstory and roles**: Quote: "who are the founders what's their backstory how do they come together how do they know each other who on the team is doing what"
- **Honesty is binary**: Quote: "anyone that's doing intentional obfuscation or misrepresentation of things in their application like revenue or traction your founder background your educational history you name it that just that's disqualifying"
- **Extraordinary claims**: Quote: "extraordinary claims on your application require extraordinary evidence"
- **Monthly versus annual revenue**: A common misrepresentation. Quote: "it's pretty common to see people kind of try to misrepresent revenue or make it look like it's monthly revenue is really annual revenue"
- **Technical talent**: Biggest variable for interview selection. Quote: "they have 5x better odds than teams that don't"
- **Tar pit with no technical founders**: Quote: "teams where there aren't technical Founders and they're working on an unlaunched tarpet idea those folks have the lowest odds"
- **No networking needed**: Quote: "we built a system that is optimized for funding complete strangers that fill out a form on our website"
- **Follow directions**: A proxy for detail-orientation. Quote: "this is one of the ways to tell how detail-oriented someone is if they just follow directions"
- **Interview questions are basic and contextual**: Pre-launch: when do you launch; regulated: regulatory strategy; many competitors: differentiation. Quote: "if you're in a space where there's many competitors we're going to ask you about your differentiation"
- **Working-relationship test**: Quote: "do I believe I can have a productive productive working relationship with these Founders"
- **Mastery of own business**: Quote: "they understand their own numbers they understand the risks they have a crisp idea on what to work on next"
- **Credible and self-aware**: Quote: "they're honest they're self-aware about what the real challenges are right like they're not in denial about this but they still are confident"
- **No rehearsed speeches**: Quote: "Founders that recite memorize speeches where they have some memorized paragraph they just shout at us oh man do we not like that"
- **No pitch-competition acting**: Quote: "not act like you're on a Shark Tank Episode or a pitch competition"
- **Feedback uptake**: Quote: "internalizing this feedback and demonstrating that you've addressed it in a future application is heavily weighted in your favor"
- **Too early or too far along are not valid**: Quote: "there's honestly no such thing as too early"
- **Do not overthink timing**: Quote: "don't overthink the decisions if something seems like it might be a good idea give it a shot"
- **Gatekeeper test**: Ask a discouraging investor whether they will invest. Quote: "if the investor is not willing to invest themselves they're simply saying they don't want to see you raise"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- In one or two concrete sentences, what is it, what does it do and who uses it? — buzzword-heavy description nobody can summarize.
- What exists today: built, demo, users, customers? — "might be built", "might have customers".
- Is there evidence people want this and do you use it yourselves? — no users, no own use.
- Who writes the code and what is each founder's role? — no technical founder.
- Why are you the ones to do this, and how did you come together? — no backstory.
- When will you launch? (pre-launch) — no launch plan.
- What is your regulatory strategy? (regulated space) — has not thought about it.
- How are you differentiated from the many competitors? — no differentiation.
- What are your numbers and what is the next thing you will work on? — does not know own numbers.
- Is this monthly or annual revenue, and who is the named customer? — misrepresentation.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Obfuscated or buzzword description — "weak applications obfuscate everything".
- Any misrepresentation of revenue, traction, background or education — "automatic disqualification".
- Non-technical founding team with an unlaunched tar pit idea — "the lowest odds".
- Unfinished application, sloppy grammar — "may not be that serious".
- Memorized or robotic interview answers, not listening to the question.
- Seeming to stretch the truth — "that's going to get you low scores".
- Treating the interview as adversarial.
- Outside coaching to be confusing or misleading.

## Green flags
- Founders left their jobs to work on it.
- Founding team with skills for a technical role at a top YC company (internships at Stripe or Airbnb count; includes biotech and hard tech).
- Clear concrete description with named users.
- Applicants with persistent progress across several applications.
- Mastery of numbers and risks, crisp next steps, honest about challenges.
- Natural conversation and real self.

## Named examples
- GitLab: its YC application as a clear, concrete description (multi-billion dollar public company, "a hundred thousand organizations").
- Cloud hosting for React apps story (fictional): the shape of a good application story.
- Co-founder matching: a way to add technical talent.
- Shark Tank: the pitch style to avoid.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the idea that too-early or too-far-along companies should wait; YC funds pre-code teams and companies with "half a million dollars in annual recurring revenue" or "a million dollars" raised.
- Contradicts the folk belief that warm intros and decks help; claims strangers filling in a form is the model.
- Contradicts heavy interview preparation advice: too much preparation backfires.
- Says applying multiple times is a plus, not a negative.
- The 5x figure is about interview selection odds at YC, not startup success.
