## One-line thesis
First-time founders fail mostly by choosing problems and users they do not care about, picking cofounders they do not know, not launching, and prioritizing sizzle (press, hiring, investors) over shipping and talking to users.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Problem you care about**: ask whether the founders would work on this for years; startups fail when motivation dies. Quote: "a lot of startups that fail basically fail because they lose motivation to continue working on their their company"
- **Picked to look good, not cared about**: red flag if the problem was chosen because it might grow fast or seems cool. Quote: "they were trying to pick a problem that they thought people might want or they thought could grow quickly or they thought might be cool"
- **Five-plus years test**: Quote: "it wasn't actually something they were willing to devote a significant amount of time like you know five years plus of their life to solving"
- **Love your users**: founders should care about the people they serve. Quote: "helping users you don't care about"
- **Twitch example of refocus**: Quote: "only when Emmett kind of really refocused the company around video game streaming did we regain the love for our users"
- **Pre-existing cofounder relationship**: Quote: "startups are so hard that it is nice to have a pre-existing relationship with your co-founder just so you have a sense of whether you're going to be able to"
- **Transparent cofounder talks**: three drama topics: performance, goals, roles. Quote: "most co-founding teams performance is my co-founder working as hard as I am goals are we trying to accomplish the same goal rules whose job is it to do engineering"
- **Resentment compounds**: Quote: "as a result resentment builds up and the relationship degrades and then when you do have the conversation turns into a fight"
- **Launch early**: launching matters less to users than to founders. Quote: "launching is nowhere near a significant event to your users as it is to you so you should move up the launch as soon as possible"
- **Cannot validate without customers**: Quote: "until you can get your product in front of customers you can't validate whether it solves their problem"
- **MVP in a month**: Quote: "in most consumer and b2b startups that we encounter it's actually possible to get some form of MVP built and launched in less than a month"
- **Regulated exception**: Quote: "in some extremely regulated markets like banking example or lending it's just really hard to launch"
- **Measure usage**: Quote: "part and parcel to building product is measuring so that you know what's being used and what's not"
- **First users must be known**: first one to five users should come from people you know. Quote: "where I get my first two users or one user or three users or five users you know that should come from people you know or you already have identified"
- **Founder with no access to the problem**: Quote: "you chose a problem where you don't know anyone who has the problem including yourself"
- **Sizzle vs steak**: Quote: "oftentimes people will prioritize sizzle over steak"
- **Cargo cult startup**: Quote: "you are pretending to be a start-up but the actual work of startups is pushing product getting in the users hands and seeing if they like it and then iterating"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Why this problem, and would you still work on it in five years? — red flag: picked because it seemed big, trendy or cool
- Do you like the people who use your product? — red flag: indifference to the user group (Justin.tv before Twitch)
- How long have you known your cofounder and what happened when you disagreed? — red flag: met recently, no history of hard moments
- Have you talked about who works how hard, goals and roles? — red flag: unspoken resentments
- What stops you launching this month? — red flag: waiting for press-ready polish (unless heavily regulated)
- Who are your first five users by name? — red flag: no one known who has the problem, not even the founder
- What do you measure about usage? — red flag: no analytics
- What did you do this week besides press, hiring, conferences, investors? — red flag: sizzle over product and user conversations

## Red flags / kill signals
- Problem chosen for market appeal without personal connection — "There was no deep connection to the problem"
- Indifference to user group — lost love for users at Justin.tv until the Twitch refocus
- Cofounder found recently — cannot assess how you will do in hard times
- Untouched topics between cofounders — resentment then fights
- Fear of launching because of press exposure
- No idea where first users will come from
- Priority on press, hiring, conferences and investors — "cargo cult in the startup"

## Green flags
- Founder is the user or loves the user (Emmett and gamers)
- Cofounders with prior relationship (friend, coworker, school project)
- Regular open, structured cofounder conversations
- MVP launched in under a month, analytics in place
- First users come from the founders' network

## Named examples
- Justin.tv and Twitch: lost love for users until refocus on video game streaming; Emmett was a gamer
- Snapchat, Instagram, WhatsApp, Uber, Lyft: nobody remembers their launch days
- Banking and lending: regulated markets where launching early is hard

## Tensions
- Says each mistake has counterexamples ("I bet you can name a start-up that has made every one of these mistakes and has still been successful") and mistake one "isn't fatal"; founders can learn to love a problem later.
- Advice to launch fast has a regulated-market exception.
- Prefers existing cofounder relationships but not required ("pre-existing relationship can be a friendship it could be a co-worker").
