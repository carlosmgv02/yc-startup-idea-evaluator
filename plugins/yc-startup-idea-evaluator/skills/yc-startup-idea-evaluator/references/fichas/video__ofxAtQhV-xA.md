## One-line thesis
In an AI platform shift, infrastructure is supply constrained, agent trust and security become the gating problem, and incumbents win by operating with both speed and scale; AI adoption should chase new insights, not efficiency.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Supply-constrained demand test**: check whether demand is consumed as fast as supply is added (not hoped-for demand). Quote: "no matter how much infrastructure you're building it's getting consumed almost instantly"
- **Early-adoption headroom**: market size argument based on a tiny user base today. Quote: "we have sub 2% of the of humans that are using agents in any kind of power user capacity"
- **Agent load multiplier**: agents consume more than humans on the same task. Quote: "an agent is 450% more consumptive on network bandwidth than a human would be"
- **Trusted delegation is the market gate**: products that let people delegate safely win; untrusted ones fail. Quote: "The difference between trusted delegation and untrusted delegation is the difference between market leadership and complete abject bankruptcy"
- **Machine-scale threats need machine-scale defenses**: Quote: "most of the attacks are happening at machine scale So you have to have the defenses at machine scale as well"
- **Agent failure modes**: refunds, rogue actions; runtime guardrails and non-human identity. Quote: "if you ask an agent for a refund it shouldn't be giving you three times the amount that you paid"
- **Bubble vs. froth**: valuations can be frothy while the shift is real; a bubble is when the shift itself is questioned. Quote: "bubble is if that secular shift itself is questioned"
- **Unlearning**: past experience as liability when judging AI markets. Quote: "one of the most important skills to learn um in AI is unlearning because your past experience can be a huge liability"
- **AI adoption for insights, not efficiency**: Quote: "The goal is not efficiency The goal is to be able to go out and make sure that we benefit from the original insights that AI generates"
- **Human bottleneck moves up the stack**: Quote: "Judgment on what to build and what not to build becomes a bottleneck"
- **Familiar, then good, then efficient**: do not start with efficiency mandates. Quote: "if you try to go efficiency first you'll never get familiar or you'll never get good"
- **Planning-cycle moat**: a full stack with layers on different cycles is hard to copy. Quote: "if you can figure that out as a company you have a very strategic mode"
- **Speed with scale**: Quote: "If you just operate at ski at speed then you're just a startup If you just operate at scale then you're just the largest company"
- **Leadership team mix**: Quote: "I try to call it a rule of thirds"
- **Business model must fit ambition**: Quote: "my ambition did not align with the with the model of the business"
- **Long time horizon**: Quote: "the ability for someone for a founder to think in a 20-year window versus a 3-month window changes the quality of decisions you make materially"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What is the safety/trust story if an agent using your product does the wrong thing? — Red flag: no runtime guardrails, identity or audit.
- Is your demand consumed as fast as you supply it, or are you hoping demand catches up? — Red flag: capacity ahead of proven usage (dot-com pattern).
- Does your AI plan target new capabilities or just 20% efficiency? — Red flag: headcount-cut framing only.
- Does the business model fit what you want to build in 10 years? — Red flag: a lifestyle model for a world-changing ambition.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Demand "hoped" to catch up to infrastructure supply — the dot-com contrast.
- Treating AI as an efficiency or layoff project: resistance and no insight.
- Founder staying in a business out of ego rather than learning (his own 17-year regret).
- Cynicism about large companies when selling to or working with them.

## Green flags
- Orders far above guidance: from a projected "a billion dollars in orders taken" to "2.3 billion in year one", then $9 billion the next year (his figures).
- Top-down mandate on a few "religion" issues, with tooling and unlimited tokens to get familiarity.
- Sceptic converts (refactor of about half a million lines to about 120,000 in two weeks, his account).
