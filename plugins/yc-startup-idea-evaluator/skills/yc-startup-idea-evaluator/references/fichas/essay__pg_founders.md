## One-line thesis
Above a basic intelligence threshold, the founder qualities that matter are determination, flexibility, imagination, naughtiness and a strong friendship between cofounders.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Determination first**: past an intelligence threshold, resilience to obstacles matters most. Quote: "as long as you're over a certain threshold of intelligence, what matters most is determination."
- **Not easily demoralized**: Quote: "You can't be the sort of person who gets demoralized easily."
- **Determination plus flexibility**: persistent about the goal, willing to change the route (running back metaphor). Quote: "The world of startups is so unpredictable that you need to be able to modify your dreams on the fly."
- **Imagination over speed**: look for the ability to produce surprising ideas, not to solve predefined problems fast. Quote: "It's not so important to be able to solve predefined problems quickly as to be able to come up with surprising new ideas."
- **Good ideas look bad at first**: an obviously good idea is already taken. Quote: "In the startup world, most good ideas seem bad initially. If they were obviously good, someone would already be doing them."
- **Right level of craziness**: Quote: "So you need the kind of intelligence that produces ideas with just the right level of craziness."
- **Naughtiness probe**: ask for a time they hacked something to their advantage (beat the system). Quote: "ask about a time when they'd hacked something to their advantage"
- **Rules that matter**: founders break rules that do not matter, not ones that do. Quote: "They delight in breaking rules, but not rules that matter."
- **Cofounders**: solo is hard, successful ones have two or three. Quote: "Empirically it seems to be hard to start a startup with just one founder."
- **Relationship strength**: founders must genuinely like each other. Quote: "They must genuinely like one another, and work well together."
- **Founder-relationship stress test**: Quote: "Startups do to the relationship between the founders what a dog does to a sock: if it can be pulled apart, it will be."

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- Tell me about a time you hacked a system to your advantage. — no example, or only rule-following or malicious hacking.
- What has been the biggest obstacle you hit and what did you do next? — gave up or has no obstacle story.
- When did you last change your plan mid-way? — rigid "don't give up on your dreams" attachment to one idea.
- How long have you known your cofounder and how do you handle disagreement? — recently met, tension unresolved.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Easily demoralized founder.
- Determination without flexibility ("don't give up on your dreams" type).
- Only one founder with no strong relationship to others (empirically hard).
- Cofounders who do not genuinely like each other.

## Green flags
- Founder who keeps coming back until the answer is yes ("he is not going away").
- Quick pivots when told to change idea.
- Ideas that seem bad but not obviously so.
- Cofounders with a long shared history who "can practically read one another's minds".

## Named examples
- WePay (Bill Clerico, Rich Aberman): determination in deals with big bureaucratic companies.
- Greplin (Daniel Gross): record flexibility, went through several ideas.
- Airbnb: idea YC thought too crazy, funded on the strength of the founders (breakfast cereal story).
- Justin.tv (Emmett Shear, Justin Kan): close friends since second grade.
- Sam Altman (Loopt): origin of the "hacked something" application question.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the "Valley myth" that intelligence is the most important founder quality.
- Admits YC thought Airbnb "too crazy" and funded on founders, not idea: founder quality can outweigh idea evaluation.
- Naughtiness is flagged as possibly redundant with imagination.
