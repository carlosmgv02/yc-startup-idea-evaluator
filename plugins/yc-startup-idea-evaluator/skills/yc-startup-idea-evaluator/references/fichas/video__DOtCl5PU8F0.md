## One-line thesis
A startup idea is a hypothesis about why a company can grow quickly, built from three parts (problem as initial conditions, solution as experiment, insight/unfair advantage as the explanation), and evaluating it means finding which part lacks evidence and what to prove next.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Definition of startup**: The evidence investors want is evidence of fast growth. Quote: "a company that is designed or created to try to grow very quickly"
- **Idea as hypothesis**: Quote: "a start-up idea is basically a hypothesis"
- **Three components**: Problem, solution, insight. Quote: "startup idea is composed of three parts"
- **Optimism over hole-poking**: The job is to find how it could win, since winners are non-obvious. Quote: "it's not to figure out what's wrong with the company but to figure out how it could possibly win"
- **Popular problem**: Many people have it. Quote: "good problems they're popular so a lot of people have the problem you want to avoid problems that there's a small number of people that have it"
- **Growing problem**: Quote: "we like problems that are growing"
- **Urgent problem**: Quote: "we like problems that are urgent ones that need to be solved very very quickly"
- **Expensive problem**: Quote: "we like problems that are really expensive to solve because if you're able to sort of solve it then you can charge a lot of money potentially"
- **Mandatory problem**: Quote: "we like problems that are mandatory"
- **Frequent problem**: Quote: "we like problems that are frequent ones that people are gonna encounter over and over and over again"
- **At least one**: Need not have all. Quote: "you don't have to have all of them"
- **Frequency gives conversion opportunities**: Quote: "I like problems and you'll find a lot of other YC partners like problems a lot that gives people a lot of opportunities to convert"
- **Motivation, ability, trigger (BJ Fogg)**: Quote: "you need to have the motivation the ability and the trigger they need to all happen at the same time"
- **Retention gap is often a trigger gap**: Quote: "you're hoping that they will somehow just remember on their own that they have the problem and to start using you"
- **Ideal numbers**: Quote: "we like markets that are growing 20% a year"
- **Law changed**: Quote: "we like problems where the law has changed"
- **Daily-use problem**: Quote: "problem is that people need to solve multiple times a day"
- **Do not start with the solution**: SISP, solution in search of a problem. Quote: "don't start here"
- **Technology-first signal**: Quote: "you try to shoehorn a problem into the solution"
- **Start from problems**: Quote: "let me see what problems people have and then I will use whatever is necessary to solve them"
- **Insight must relate to growth**: Quote: "you have to have an unfair advantage that explains why you're going to grow quickly"
- **Insight is required**: Quote: "without that last sort of explanation I can't use my imagination"
- **Five unfair advantage types, at least one**: Quote: "you want at least one and it's nice if you can have two or three but for most of you it's probably just one"
- **Founder advantage: one in ten**: Quote: "are you one in ten of all the people in the world who can solve this problem are you super expert"
- **Common job titles are not an advantage**: Quote: "there's a lot of product managers at Google"
- **Market advantage is weak alone**: Quote: "if this is your only company advantage then it's one of the weakest ones that you could have"
- **Shrinking market**: Quote: "if you're in a market that is stagnating or shrinking then you're gonna have investors worried about the long-term viability"
- **10x product**: Quote: "is your product 10x better than the competition"
- **Obvious superiority**: Quote: "someone should be able to look at your product and go like oh this is so much better than everything else I've ever seen"
- **2x or 3x is not enough**: Quote: "that's nice but it's not enough for an investor to go like oh this is a slam dunk"
- **Paid acquisition discounted**: Quote: "if paid acquisition is the only way that you were able to grow your company then I'm gonna discount that channel of growth greatly"
- **Why paid fades**: Quote: "you're gonna attract a lot of competitors into the space and that advantage is going to quickly dwindling"
- **Free acquisition and word of mouth**: Quote: "my favorite companies the ones that become really great are the ones that can grow by word-of-mouth"
- **Monopoly means getting stronger**: Quote: "as your company grows is it more difficult for you to be defeated by competitors do you get stronger"
- **Network effects**: Quote: "as my network grows the strength of my company and the value of the product or service also grows with it"
- **Threshold versus miracle belief**: Quote: "there's the threshold belief which is like what's the default just for them to even succeed"
- **Miracle belief is what matters**: Quote: "it's a miracle belief that like oh my god if I believe that they can do this that actually going to be able to take off really well"
- **B2B and Enterprise: sales ability is the miracle**: Quote: "success will be determined by how well you can do sales how well you can tell the story how well you can actually convince customers"
- **Idea stage can get in**: Quote: "there's lots of great examples of companies who actually got accepted just with an idea"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- How many people have this problem, is that number growing, and how fast? — small or flat population.
- Is the problem urgent, mandatory, expensive or frequent (which of these)? — none of them.
- Did you start from the problem or from a technology you wanted to use? — solution in search of a problem.
- What is your insight: why will you grow faster than everyone else? — no explanation or an explanation unrelated to growth.
- Are you one in ten who could solve this, and why? — a generic job title.
- Is the product 10x better and in what measurable way? — 2x or 3x.
- How do you acquire customers without paying for them? — paid ads only.
- Does the company get harder to beat as it grows? — no network effect or scale advantage.
- What is the miracle belief I need, and what evidence do you have for it? — only evidence that it can be built.
- What is the trigger that brings the user back? — no reminders or hooks.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Solution in search of a problem (new technology first) — "much more difficult way to grow the company".
- Small number of people with the problem.
- Stagnating or shrinking market.
- Only paid acquisition (CAC and LTV on ad channels) — discounted heavily.
- Marginal improvement of 2x or 3x.
- No explanation of why the company wins ("I have a problem I'm solving it").
- Retention problem with no trigger or reminders.
- Founder advantage claimed from a very common background.

## Green flags
- Problem that is popular, growing, urgent, expensive, mandatory or frequent (at least one, ideally several).
- Law or regulation change creating forced demand.
- Founder one in ten (special expertise or patent).
- Product demonstrably 10x better (faster, cheaper).
- Free acquisition: audience built in advance, embeds that spread the product, word of mouth.
- Network effects, winner-takes-all dynamics.
- Evidence on the miracle belief (for B2B: sales).

## Named examples
- Zenefits: Parker [Conrad, surname not in transcript], a single non-technical founder, got in with an idea.
- Reddit: no code written at acceptance; forced to pivot right away.
- Wufoo: entered YC without a line of code; drag-and-drop form builder; 10x faster than builders and "a hundred X faster" than programmers; blog audience of "a hundred thousand developers" before launch; embedded forms spread the product; raised "118 thousands" total, returns "over thirty thousand percent".
- YC itself: problem (hard to raise money without knowing a VC), solution (open application), unfair advantages from founders, market (software cheaper), product, acquisition (essays audience), and network growing.
- Affordable Care Act: law change created healthcare startups.
- Facebook, Slack: multiple-times-a-day use.
- BJ Fogg (Stanford): motivation, ability, trigger model.

## Tensions
- Contradicts the "tell me what's wrong" investor posture: "I will never tell you that I do not like your idea".
- Contradicts the myth that YC only funds traction.
- Nuances "do things that don't scale" by pairing it with a free acquisition advantage.
- Threshold belief (can they build it) deemed not the main question, against the engineering-first view.
- Discounts paid acquisition proofs (CAC and LTV) that many investors accept.
- Market growth of 20% a year is "one of the weakest" advantages on its own, even though it is listed as ideal.
