## One-line thesis
Startups win when technology shifts fast and cost and cycle time collapse; pick a big new thing you hold contrarian but data-backed conviction in, find the few people who share it, and ignore the "AI ends startups" narrative.

## Evaluation heuristics
- **Cluster timing**: great startups cluster when the ecosystem shifts and incumbents lose their advantage. Quote: "the great startups tend to cluster when the ecosystem shifts and incumbents lose a lot of their advantage"
- **Cost and cycle time**: startups win when costs fall and cycles shorten. Quote: "startups tend to win when um the sort of like technology landscape is moving very quickly uh when costs are coming down when cycle times are short"
- **Contrarian conviction with data**: Quote: "find the things that you can develop like reasonable conviction in that people decide like the conventional wisdom is they're just wrong"
- **Tolerate being dismissed for years**: Quote: "be okay with it taking a long time and having people be like very you know very frustratingly wrong and dismissive"
- **Crowded-idea warning**: the same startup as everyone else brings hype and funding but rarely big outcomes. Quote: "if you're starting the same startup as everybody else it's like you get a lot of hype and you can raise a lot of money but it's it's sort of like those are much less frequently the big option the big outcomes"
- **Hunt new exponentials**: Quote: "There's got to be new exponentials forming right now"
- **Find your people**: Quote: "only um 50 people in the world believe that AGI was possible but it was okay because 45 of them worked at OpenAI"
- **Consensus as a bad sign**: Quote: "if everybody believes it that's also like a bad a bad sign"
- **Lonely founder signal**: Quote: "if you can't find anybody else that shares your belief you should pay attention to that"
- **Clear vision, unclear first steps**: Quote: "if the the kind of like highest level vision is clear but the first few steps are very unclear that's not a bad thing"
- **But you must still step forward**: Quote: "you do still have to take some steps forward imperfect though they may be"
- **Skills shift from experience to tool fluency; taste still matters**: Quote: "taste and agency and understanding of kind of like the physics of business"
- **Real vs fake moats**: Quote: "like a good network effect or sort of a good moat looks like versus a fake one"
- **Network effects of YC itself**: Quote: "the premium on doing YC is bigger now than it's like ever been before"
- **Do the work**: Quote: "you can now do three months of work in 17 minutes but you better just go do three months of work in three months of work"
- **Inference demand uncapped**: Quote: "demand for sufficiently high quality intelligence at a sufficiently low price is effectively uncapped"
- **Compute resale as a fallback**: Quote: "you either die a model company or uh live long enough to sell inference"
- **Be helpful to many people**: Quote: "find a way to be like mildly helpful to a lot of people"
- **Avoid cynicism**: Quote: "put all of your energy into building stuff and like resist the easy shots"

## Questions a YC partner would ask (derived from this source)
- What do you believe that most experts think is wrong, and what data supports it? — Red flag: your idea is what everyone agrees on, or you have no data backing it.
- Who else believes it and works with you? — Red flag: nobody; or a thousand copycats.
- What new exponential makes this possible only now? — Red flag: the answer is just "AI" without a specific cost or capability shift.
- If a coding agent builds your first version in minutes, what is the hard part? — Red flag: the product is only that first version.
- What would you attempt if you had "experts in every field working together"? — Red flag: the ambition fits a prompt, not a company.

## Red flags / kill signals
- Idea identical to what everybody is building (hype without big outcomes).
- Founder who defers to "end of history" narratives ("permanent underclass").
- Vision with no forward steps: "you can kind of never make any forward progress".
- Fake moat: no network effect or defensibility beyond the model.

## Green flags
- Reasonable conviction, a small group of misfits who share it, and increasing data points.
- Hard-tech or very ambitious scope enabled by agents (hard tech at YC from about 5-10% to 15-25%, per the interviewer).
- Founder helping others and building a network over years.

## Named examples
- Looped (Altman's YC S05 startup) and Posterous (interviewer's): examples that now take a coding-agent prompt.
- OpenAI: early AGI belief, years of ridicule, no competitors during that time.
- Stripe: how Altman met Greg Brockman (dinner to recruit).
- Hugging Face incident: AI agent breaking out of a sandbox; he calls it an alignment and security failure.
- Token usage anecdote: token leader at about 100,000 tokens/month then; hundreds of billions now (his figures).

## Tensions
- Contradicts the "frontier labs or permanent underclass" meme; says startups matter more.
- Contradicts crowd wisdom: popular ideas draw hype but not big outcomes; yet "if everybody believes it that's a bad sign" has no operational test.
- Cannot verify some numbers (e.g. "90,000x" inference growth "by our accounts internal to YC", the interviewer quips; he doubts it will continue).
- Self-interested speaker: OpenAI CEO says inference demand is effectively uncapped and startups win.
